# B1G-M Backbone — how the state guides drive the A7 rebuild

**Ruled by Croix, 20 September 2026:** *"Grade 8 is the spine. Yes to all four."* The four were:
(1) Grade 8 B1G-M is the spec, Grade 7 B1G-M is the prerequisite map; (2) the guide's numeric caps
become hard checks; (3) the reference sheet follows the memorize/provide split literally;
(4) every distractor is recorded in the TE by its guide-named error. Recorded in HOUSE STYLE
§13b(iv). Source record: `B1G-M READING NOTES.md` (every benchmark, every item worked).

---

## 1. The spine and the map

- **Spine:** the 40 MA.8 benchmarks, in the Math Nation accelerated book's order (HOUSE STYLE §10).
  Every lesson names the MA.8 benchmark it serves; a lesson that serves none is enrichment and is
  flagged, not silently built.
- **Map:** for each MA.8 benchmark, the Grade 8 guide's *Previous Benchmarks* names what a student
  must already own. Where that is one of the 17 carried MA.7 benchmarks, the A7 lesson teaches it
  as a prerequisite inside the Grade 8 lesson (or the unit before). Where it is a MA.7 benchmark
  the course DROPS (Source of Truth Part 0), the TE names the gap and the lesson does not depend
  on it.
- The guide's own instruction stands: benchmarks are *not* taught in the order they appear in the
  areas-of-emphasis table.

## 2. Hard caps → `capcheck` (ruling 2)

Every generated item carries its benchmark code in the generator (never on the student page,
§13b ruling 6). `capcheck` reads each item's benchmark and its numbers and **fails the build**
on any of these. Each row is the guide's clarification, verbatim in spirit.

| Benchmark | Cap |
|---|---|
| NSO.1.1 | expression has exactly ONE arithmetic operation; root or π only |
| NSO.1.2 | no *e*; roots are square or cube only |
| NSO.1.3, AR.1.1 | integer exponents only, never fractional; NSO.1.3 bases rational |
| NSO.1.5, NSO.1.6 | for + or −, the two exponents differ by ≤ 2 |
| NSO.1.6 | a product/quotient answer carries the LEAST sig-digit count of its operands |
| NSO.1.7 | ≤ 6 steps; square radicands are perfect squares ≤ 225; cube radicands perfect cubes in [−125, 125] |
| AR.1.2 | at least one factor is a monomial (no binomial × binomial) |
| AR.2.2 | forms px ± q ▷ r or p(x ± q) ▷ r only |
| AR.2.3 | p a whole number, perfect square ≤ 225; q an integer, perfect cube in [−125, 125]; x² or x³ already isolated |
| AR.3.3, AR.3.4, AR.4.3 | slope-intercept form only (any of y = mx + b, y = ax + b, y = b + mx); never point-slope or standard |
| AR.3.5 | never asks to compare the slopes/intercepts of two equations |
| F.1.1 | domain/range given as a list (discrete) or an inequality (continuous), never an inequality for a discrete set |
| F.1.3 | continuous functions only |
| GR.1.1, GR.1.2 | radicand ≤ 225; GR.1.2 never uses the distance formula |
| GR.2.1 | reflection, translation, rotation only; a translation moves in ONE direction |
| GR.2.3 | reflection lines: axes or lines parallel to them; rotations about the origin, 90/180/270/360, direction stated; dilations centered at the origin |
| DP.1.1 | ≤ 20 data points |
| DP.2.1–2.3 | repetition = 2, except coin tosses; card/marble draws WITH replacement; ≤ 6 outcomes per repetition when listing |
| 7.AR.2.2 (carried) | forms px ± q = r, p(x ± q) = r |
| 7.DP.1.4 (carried) | ≤ 6 categories |
| 7.GR.2.2, 2.3 (carried) | never solve for the radius |

`capcheck` is a §13c check: it names the item, the cap, and the offending value, and it runs
on the question banks retroactively. Existing Unit 1–2 banks get audited against it before
any Unit 3 work.

## 3. Reference sheet (ruling 3)

The classroom Reference Sheet carries **exactly** what the guides say students are not expected to
memorize, and nothing the guides say they must know cold.

**ON the sheet:** slope formula (FAST prints it) · Triangle Inequality · Triangle Sum (both FAST) ·
the FAST conversion tables, line for line · simple interest I = Prt (7.AR.3.1, "provide the
formula") · C = πd, C = 2πr, A = πr² (7.GR.1.3–1.4 "not expected to memorize"; π ≈ 3.14, 22/7,
355/113 — these approximations students ARE expected to know, so they print as reminders, not
as a crutch) · area of trapezoid, parallelogram, rhombus (7.GR.1.1) · cylinder net → SA and
V = Bh = πr²h (7.GR.2.1–2.3) · F ↔ C conversion (AR.3.5, "memorization is not an expectation").

**OFF the sheet:** Pythagorean Theorem and its converse (GR.1.1–1.3, "expectation is to
memorize") · y = mx + b · Laws of Exponents · scientific-notation rules · exterior-angle
relationship · (n − 2)·180° (students DEVELOP it, GR.1.6) · angle-pair relationships · percent
change / percent error (7.AR.3.1 is dropped from this course).

Two consequences, both to say to students: the Grade 8 FAST sheet has none of the circle or
cylinder formulas — and the Grade 8 FAST never asks those benchmarks, so nothing is lost; and the
sheet comes into the unit exam only as their own notes (ruling 11).

## 4. Named distractors in the TE (ruling 4)

§16 rule 4 is mandatory. From now on every whiteboard distractor and every assessment distractor is
recorded in the TE in this form, one line per wrong option:

    B  — combined the discounts (25% then 10% read as 35%)        [7.AR.3.1 misconception 5]

The bracket cites the guide's misconception by benchmark and position in the notes, so the error
is the state's named error, not one invented on the day. A distractor with no guide-named error
is allowed only when the TE says what error it IS — "arithmetic slip" is not an error name and
fails the check (`distractorcheck`, §13c: every non-key option on a whiteboard or assessment has a
named-error line in the TE).

The catalogue, by benchmark, is in `B1G-M READING NOTES.md` under each *Misconceptions* line.
The ones that recur across the Grade 8 spine:

- **Exponents:** multiplying the exponent into the base (6³ = 18); negative exponent read as a
  negative number; adding exponents when multiplying bases that differ.
- **Scientific notation:** (1.3×10³) + (3.4×10⁵) = 4.7×10⁸; (2×10⁴)(3×10⁵) = 6×10²⁰; leaving
  12×10⁹; "10⁴ times bigger" read as "4 times"; multiplying the exponent when scaling.
- **Equations/inequalities:** distributing to one term only; dropping the negative across a
  subtraction; symbol not flipped on ÷ by a negative; open/closed circle swapped; shading in the
  direction the symbol points; expecting x = constant when the answer is "no solution."
- **Roots:** squaring as ×2, cubing as ×3; √p with only the positive root; "negatives have no
  cube root"; ∛ read as √.
- **Linear:** rise and run inverted; m and b swapped; "not proportional because the origin isn't
  shown"; every straight line called proportional; constant rate of change taken as constant
  ratio.
- **Systems:** substituting into one equation only; coincident lines read as "only the drawn
  points"; parallel lines read as "one solution far away."
- **Functions:** repeated x (different y) missed; repeated x with SAME y wrongly called
  non-function; domain/range swapped; input/output swapped.
- **Pythagorean/angles:** hypotenuse misidentified; theorem applied to a non-right triangle;
  distance across an axis subtracted instead of added; exterior angle taken as the reflex angle;
  complementary/supplementary swapped; extra diagonals over-counting triangles.
- **Transformations:** wrong axis; one vertex moved; clockwise/counterclockwise swapped; k < 1
  called "2 instead of ½."
- **Data:** positive/negative association swapped; one descriptor only ("positive" for "strong
  positive linear"); line of fit forced through every point or the first and last.
- **Probability:** {red, red, blue} → P(red) = ½; GY and YG counted once; all events assumed
  equally likely; theoretical probability read as what WILL happen.

## 5. Wording the state takes a position on (goes into §7 / §16 on the next HOUSE STYLE pass)

- No PEMDAS as a mnemonic; teach same-rank operations left to right.
- Do not emphasize cross-multiplying; write the two ratios with an equal sign.
- Vertical line test "treated with caution" — teach the definition (each input, one output).
- Exponent laws derived one at a time from expansion, never handed as a list.
- 4(x + 7) = 12: divide by 4 or distribute; both are shown, neither is "the" way.
- Slope: "vertical change divided by horizontal change" alongside "rise over run."
- Equations appear as y = ax + b and y = b + mx, not only y = mx + b.
- Variables vary; o, i, l are never used.
- Same-type rule: fractions in, fractions out; "exact value" means the fraction or the π form.
- Every real-world probability item uses P(event) notation.

## 6. Item shapes the guides use that our banks lack

Multiselect (stem does not say how many) · error analysis ("is Kevin right? what is his error?")
· always / sometimes / never · "choose the measure, compute it, justify it" · figure orientation as
the difficulty (cylinder on its side; rotated similar triangles) · two-sided stem-and-leaf ·
matching grid (expressions to equivalents) · a system given with candidate points, not to solve.
Each gets a whiteboard-round template in the rebuilt generators; multiselect and error analysis
are the two the FAST release also leans on (Source of Truth Part 4).
