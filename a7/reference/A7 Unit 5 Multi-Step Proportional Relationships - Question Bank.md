audit against Math Nation found, what changed from the book, and the answers.

**This is not a handout and it is not the teacher's edition.** Under ruling 26 the teacher's
edition is four pages read in twenty minutes; this is the reference behind it, read once when

## 5.01  Scale Drawings

*MA.7.GR.1.5*

**Variation.**  Boards 1–3 find k, going over 1, under 1, then a fraction; 4 asks k as a vote; 5 reverses the given (k and the original, asked for the copy). The pattern breaks at 6, the one measurement a scale factor leaves alone. 7 asks whether a copy is a copy at all; 8 reads a two-unit scale per 1 unit; 9 puts the direction in writing. The independent set walks the same path in six: k over 1, k under 1, k from two pairs, a side from k, a map distance, and back to the map.

**Read this once, when you plan the unit.**

- This lesson opens Unit 5 and is a 43-minute Wednesday. Built from the benchmark and the B1G-M first, then read against Math Nation 5.1 (the unit audit, §1–§4). The book's lesson is the same idea with a grid and a ruler; here nothing is measured, every length is printed, because a measured key is what broke the book's own 5.4.4 (audit D7).
- The B1G-M's named misconceptions for 7.GR.1.5 are each a board: the proportion set up backwards and 'k is always greater than 1' (boards 2 and 4); scaling an angle like a length (board 6, the boundary); the additive error — 'to get from 5 to 2, take away 3' — is a distractor on boards 4 and 6 and a named wrong board on 1, 3 and 8.
- Board 7 is the book's 'are these scale copies?' check (5.4.3, HW L3 Q8–10), with sides of its own: every pair of corresponding sides must give the same k. Unit 6 (similar triangles) leans on exactly this board.
- Wednesday is 43 minutes: the cut is boards 3 and 7 and the IXL block, never board 9; the timing table totals 53 by house rule.

**What the audit against Math Nation found.**

- Every item re-derived by sympy at build time (mathcheck: 0 findings).
- Math Nation 5.1 read against this lesson (A7 Unit 5 Audit): its ruler-and-grid tasks are not reused (printed lengths only); its 'are these scale copies?' shape is board 7; the audit's defect D14 (HW L1 Q7 keyed 3 where the words say ⅓) is the direction mistake this lesson's board 9 names.
- B1G-M 7.GR.1.5 misconceptions verified against the boards: proportion backwards (boards 2, 4, 9); k always greater than 1 (board 2); an angle scaled (board 6).

**What changed from the book.**

- 5.1.2–5.1.3 Exploration with a ruler and a grid → Notes I–III, taught from the front, every length printed.

## 5.02  Scale Factor: Perimeter and Area

*MA.7.GR.1.5*

**Variation.**  Boards 1–2 keep k = 2 and move from perimeter to area; 3 takes k under 1; 4 asks the area as a vote; 5 makes k a decimal. The pattern breaks at 6, where k comes from perimeters and the area ratio is asked. 7 is a triangle, 8 the extra number, 9 the reason in writing. The independent set walks the same path: perimeter × k, area × k², k under 1, both on one rectangle, k from areas, the area ratio from perimeters.

**Read this once, when you plan the unit.**

- Two book lessons in one day (5.2 + 5.3, the plan's own merge). Built from the benchmark's clarification 2 and the B1G-M, then read against Math Nation 5.2–5.3 (the unit audit): the book's triangle and trapezoid copies are board 7; its 'some students forget to square the scale factor' (5.3.3) is boards 2, 4 and 6; its 'the perimeter is one-dimensional just as a side length is' (the Assessment Guide) is Notes I.
- The B1G-M's named misconception for this benchmark — scaling area like length — is a distractor on both diagnostics (C on board 4, B on board 6) and the named wrong board on every area board. The boundary (board 6) hands the room a k that came from perimeters and asks for the area ratio: the whole lesson in one vote.
- k from two areas needs a square root. Every area ratio here is a perfect square (9, 16, 25, ¼, ¹⁄₁₆) — Unit 4's radicals, one step, never an approximation (outline decision 3; the book's one such item, Practice L3 Q8, is a perfect square too).
- The perimeter and the area of board 7's triangle are both 24 on purpose: the two answers (12 and 6) show the two rules apart on one figure.

**What the audit against Math Nation found.**

- Every item re-derived by sympy at build time (mathcheck: 0 findings); every square root is of a perfect square.
- Math Nation 5.2–5.3 read against this lesson (A7 Unit 5 Audit): its Practice L2 Q10 key confuses the perimeter and area rows (294/84 = 3.5, not 12.25 — audit D1), which is exactly board 6's trap; its 5.3.4 Q1c swaps actual and drawing (D8). Neither item is reused.
- B1G-M 7.GR.1.5 'scaling area like length' verified as the keyed error on boards 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8 and the bank MC.

**What changed from the book.**

- 5.2.1 and 5.3.1 Notice-and-Wonder warm-ups → four retrieval questions, with the grade-6 perimeter and area in the prior-grade slot.
- 5.2.2–5.2.3 (perimeter on a grid, partner exploration) → Notes I, taught from the front with one rectangle.
- 5.3.2–5.3.3 (area under scaling; 'forget to square') → Notes II with the four-copies grid, and boards 2, 4, 6.
- 5.2.4 and 5.3.5 (triangle and trapezoid copies) → board 7, a triangle with its own numbers.
- 5.2.5 and 5.3.6 Wrap-Ups → the independent set and IXL under rulings 10 and 21; board 9 is the exit evidence.

## 5.06  Converting Length Across Systems; Currency

*MA.7.AR.3.3*

**Variation.**  Boards 1–2 take one ratio both ways; 3–4 the next ratio, the second as a vote; 5 chains inside customary then across. The pattern breaks at 6, where three conversions run in a row and the estimate is the only check. 7 is a currency chain, 8 the extra number, 9 the error in writing. The independent set walks the same path: the four length ratios, a chain with an estimate, a currency round trip.

**Read this once, when you plan the unit.**

- Two book lessons in one day (5.6 + 5.7; the book's 5.5 Mathlandia is not on the plan and its currency idea is Notes III). Built from the benchmark and the B1G-M, then read against Math Nation (the unit audit): the book prints 1.609 and a dozen ratios the sheet does not have; its own assessment uses the sheet's. This lesson uses the sheet's values and no other — outline decision 1, taken.
- The sheet gives both directions (1 in = 2.54 cm and 1 cm = 0.39 in), so every conversion here is a multiplication by the ratio whose 1 is the unit you have. A student who divides by the other ratio instead gets the sheet's other answer (20 ÷ 2.54 = 7.87 against 20 × 0.39 = 7.8): accept both, as FAST does, whenever the work shows which ratio was used. The keys print the multiplication; the TE says where the division lands.
- The B1G-M's misconception for this benchmark — the ratio the wrong way round — is distractor C on the diagnostic and the named wrong board everywhere, and the remedy is the estimate: say which unit is bigger before the calculator. Board 6 is built so only the estimate catches the error.
- Board 7 is the Assessment Guide's own shape (a two-hop currency chain with dated rates), with our rates. Every currency rate is printed on the item and dated; none is real.

**What the audit against Math Nation found.**

- Math Nation 5.6–5.7 read against this lesson (A7 Unit 5 Audit): its 1.609 km, 2.2 lb and other off-sheet ratios are not used; its Mathlandia currency is replaced by printed, dated rates in the Assessment Guide's own shape.
- B1G-M 7.AR.3.3 'the inverted ratio' verified as the keyed error on boards 2, 3, 4, 8, 9 and both bank MCs; the estimate is the remedy on Notes II, boards 6 and 9.

## 5.08  Converting Mass, Area and Volume

*MA.7.AR.3.3*

**Variation.**  Boards 1–3 take one ratio each direction — mass, then volume both ways; 4 asks a square unit as a vote; 5 chains fluid ounces through cups. The pattern breaks at 6, where the familiar 100 is wrong. 7 converts and then compares, 8 is the extra number, 9 the cube in writing. The independent set walks the same path: mass both ways, volume, a square unit, fluid ounces through cups, square meters.

**Read this once, when you plan the unit.**

- Two book lessons in one day (5.8 + 5.9). Built from the benchmark and the B1G-M, then read against Math Nation (the unit audit): the book prints 2.2 lb per kg and a fluid-ounce-to-milliliter ratio the FAST sheet does not have. This lesson uses the sheet's 0.454 and 2.204 and sends fluid ounces through cups — the audit's finding, outline decision 1.
- The lesson's trap is 5.02–03's k² wearing different clothes: 1 ft = 12 in, so 1 ft² = 144 in². The two days are built to rhyme, and the warm-up puts them side by side (Q2 and Q4). Board 4 is the vote; board 6 is the boundary, where the metric 100 feels like it should survive squaring.
- The sheet gives both directions for mass and volume, so a student who divides by the other ratio gets the sheet's other answer (50 ÷ 2.204 = 22.69 against 50 × 0.454 = 22.7): accept both, as FAST does, when the work shows which ratio was used.
- Ounce and fluid ounce are two units that share a word — the vocabulary slide says so, and board 5's 453.6 is the student who used 28.35 g on a volume.

**What the audit against Math Nation found.**

- Every item re-derived by sympy at build time (mathcheck: 0 findings); every ratio is the FAST reference sheet's (Source of Truth, the conversions block), checked value by value; fl oz → mL is never printed.
- Math Nation 5.8–5.9 read against this lesson (A7 Unit 5 Audit): its 2.2 lb/kg and its fl-oz-to-mL line are not used; its ounce-versus-fluid-ounce confusion (D5 in the audit) is the vocabulary slide and board 5's named wrong answer.
- B1G-M 7.AR.3.3 'the inverted ratio' verified as the keyed error on boards 1, 2, 3, 8; 'the ratio once on a square unit' on boards 4, 6, 9 and both bank MCs.

**What changed from the book.**

**Read this once, when you plan the unit.**
