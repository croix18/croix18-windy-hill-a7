# Unit Audit — the ON-LEVEL Math Nation book (M7)

> **Whose book this is about.** Every unit number below is an **on-level** Math Nation unit —
> M7's book, not A7's. The two courses share a publisher and not a unit order: two-step linear
> equations are **Unit 10** on-level and **Unit 1** accelerated, which is exactly what several of
> the findings turn on. It ships inside the A7 packages because the finding in each entry is
> about the mathematics and is worth an accelerated teacher's five minutes — but if you are
> teaching A7 and a unit number here does not match your book, that is why, and it is not an
> error. A7's own sequence is in *SCOPE AND SEQUENCE 2026-2027*.

A standing check for tools Math Nation borrows from later in the book, content it teaches beyond
the benchmark, and anything above grade level. Run **before** the unit is built, and the findings
come to you as a note — not buried in a teacher's edition you open the morning of.

---

## The general rule for this book

Sequenced straight through, **everything algebraic lives in Units 7 through 11**:

| Unit | What it carries |
|---|---|
| 2 | probability, equivalent forms |
| 3 | operations with rational numbers |
| 4 | area, quadrilaterals, circles |
| 5 | probability (simulation) |
| 7 | percents, ratios, proportions |
| 8 | order of operations |
| 9 | equivalent expressions |
| 10 | **two-step equations and inequalities** |
| 11 | **proportional relationships**, circle area |

So the check is nearly mechanical: **before Unit 7, any lesson that asks a student to write or
solve an equation, set up a proportion, or manipulate an expression containing a variable is
borrowing a tool the book has not handed them yet.** Everything from Unit 7 on is fair game.

## Findings so far

### 1. Unit 2 Lesson 04 — repeating decimal to fraction. **Confirmed, hard block.**

The 10x − x method is solving a linear equation. Equations are **Unit 10** — eight units later.

It is also not required. MA.7.NSO.1.2 carries **no clarifications**, and the state's only example
runs the other direction (Justin computes 17/3, gets 5.6666666667, is he right?). Nothing asks
students to go backwards, and nothing asks for the algebraic method. Converting a repeating
decimal to a fraction is the classic proof that repeating decimals are rational, which is
**MA.8.NSO.1.1** — accelerated's benchmark, not yours.

The tell that it is an error rather than a choice: in the accelerated book, two-step linear
equations are **Unit 1**. Same publisher, same content, different unit order, and it only breaks
in one course.

*Status:* Croix taught the n/9 pattern instead and had students annotate their study guides. The
test only uses repeats the pattern handles. **Left in place for this administration.** Options
for next time are in the compression pass — cut the direction entirely, or keep the pattern and
drop the algebra.

### 2. Unit 4 Lesson 05 — exploring circles. **Confirmed, soft.**

The lesson rests on recognizing a proportional relationship — C ÷ d comes out the same for every
circle. That is **MA.7.AR.4.1 and MA.7.AR.4.3**, which live in **Unit 11**.

Softer than the first one, and worth saying so: the lab itself runs on grade-6 ratio reasoning —
measure, divide, notice the quotient barely moves. Students can do that without ever having met
the phrase *constant of proportionality*. Only the formal framing needs Unit 11. Teach it as "the
answer keeps coming out the same" and nothing is missing.

Math Nation lists the coherence for this lesson as **N/A in both directions**, which is simply
wrong — it has a clear prerequisite and a clear successor.

### 3. Unit 4 Lesson 03 — regular polygons. **Above grade.**

One of their pentagon items cannot be solved without the Pythagorean theorem to get the apothem.
That is **MA.8.GR.1.1**, grade 8. Rebuilt with the apothem given.

### 4. Unit 4 circle area is split across two units.

The district pairs **Unit 4 Lessons 8–10 with Unit 11 Lesson 9** for MA.7.GR.1.4. Taught
sequentially, students meet three quarters of circle area in Unit 4 and the rest six units later.
Worth deciding deliberately: pull 11.9 forward into Unit 4, or leave the gap and plan to revisit.

## Why the first two slipped

My prerequisite note recorded the **content** a lesson builds on, not the **technique** it uses.
Unit 2 Lesson 04's note reads *"6.NSO.3.5 — equivalent forms limited to terminating decimals,"*
which is true and useless: the thing that actually breaks is the algebra, and algebra is not
content the lesson is *about*.

Unit 4 Lesson 05's note did say *"Math Nation lists this as N/A; it is not"* — so it was found. It
went into the teacher's edition rather than to Croix, which is the same as not finding it.

**Both fixes are in the check below.**

---

## The check, from here on

Run against the Math Nation source **before building the unit**:

1. **Technique, not just content.** For every worked example, list the moves a student must
   already own — not the topic, the *moves*. Solving for a variable is a move. Cross-multiplying
   is a move. Then locate each move in the book. Anything located later in the book is a finding.
2. **Scope.** Pull the benchmark's clarifications and examples from the state course description.
   Content beyond them is optional, and optional content that costs a day is a candidate to cut.
3. **Grade level.** Anything that needs a grade 8 benchmark is a finding, whatever the book says.
4. **Their coherence claims.** Math Nation's "builds on / leads to" fields are unreliable — N/A
   twice in Unit 4 alone where a real prerequisite exists. Derive them; do not read them.
5. **Their answer key.** Work every answer independently. It has been wrong outright.
6. **Split benchmarks.** Note any benchmark whose lessons live in two different units.

Findings go to Croix as a short note **before the build starts**, not into the teacher's edition.
