## The unit's standing decisions (each reversible)

1. **Where the class stands.** Unit 11 graphed a line from a table of values (x = 0 first, then two more; the third point
   is the check), called the number that multiplies x "the coefficient of x", and wrote a story as (the amount per 1) · x +
   (the start). Unit 7 taught the proportional relationship y = px, its constant of proportionality ("the amount of y for
   every 1 unit of x"), the three tests (table, graph, description) and the equation's, "linear relationship" (its graph is
   a straight line), and "isolate" and "solve for". The class has not heard "slope" or "y-intercept".
2. **Three words, each named once and then used every day.** *Rate of change* (10.01): the amount y changes for every 1
   unit that x increases; in a linear relationship it is constant. *Slope* (10.02+03): the rate of change of a line, the
   change in y divided by the change in x between any two points on it — "rise over run" and "the vertical change divided
   by the horizontal change" are both said (the state sanctions both). *y-intercept* (10.05): the value of y where the line
   crosses the y-axis, which is the value of y when x is 0; the line passes through (0, b). A key accepts "4" or "(0, 4)".
3. **Proportional is the special case.** A linear relationship is also proportional when y is 0 when x is 0: its line
   passes through the origin. Two reasons are accepted everywhere: "y is 5 when x is 0" (or "y is 0 when x is 0"), and
   Unit 7's "y ÷ x is the same for every pair". In 10.01 every relationship is given as linear (the benchmark's words);
   deciding whether a table is linear at all is Unit 12's (12.08+09, MA.8.F.1.2 with MA.8.AR.3.1). A graph that is
   plainly curved may appear as a wrong choice: through the origin, not straight, not proportional.
4. **The slope formula is words first.** "Slope is the change in y divided by the change in x. Subtract in the same
   order." The formula m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) is shown once, in 10.02+03 Notes III, and on the Reference Sheet; no
   question needs its notation. (The Grade 8 FAST reference sheet prints this formula, "where m = slope": it is the
   one thing in this unit that the state's sheet carries, and the unit's Reference Sheet says so.)
5. **A table's x-values do not always go up by 1** (the state's own item, slope from a table, was answered correctly by 26
   percent). From 10.02+03 on, every lesson has a table whose x-steps are not 1, and 10.02+03, 10.04, 10.05, 10.07+08,
   10.09 and 10.10 each have one whose steps are unequal. Where such a table is a multiple-choice question about its
   slope (10.02+03 board 3, 10.04 board 5), "the change in y alone" is one of the wrong options.
6. **Which quantity is x and which is y is said in every question** (Unit 7's rule): a table names its rows, a graph its
   axes ("y versus x"), a slope carries its unit as "y-unit per x-unit".
7. **Graphs have readable points, and an axis scale is not always 1.** "Read the scales first" (Unit 7's line): a slope
   on a scaled graph is found from coordinates, never by counting squares. A real-world graph is drawn in Quadrant I and
   starts at x = 0.
8. **Signs.** A line that rises from left to right has a positive slope, a line that falls has a negative slope, a
   horizontal line has slope 0. A vertical line is on no page (its slope is Algebra 1's).
9. **Slope-intercept form only** (the benchmark's boundary): y = mx + b, also written y = b + mx and with the letters of
   a context (c = 20n + 35). No point-slope form, no standard form on the test. When no y-intercept is in sight: "Substitute
   a point and solve for b" (the B1G-M's method; Unit 11's equation), or step back to x = 0 in a table whose steps allow it.
10. **One exception for IXL.** Croix's IXL plan puts "Graph a line from an equation in standard form" (7MZ) on 10.06. The
    lesson gives it one Notes block, one whiteboard question, one question of the Independent Set and one question in
    each bank — an equation in another form is graphed from a table of values — and the Review and the test never ask
    it. (The benchmark's boundary keeps standard form out of what the state expects; it is here because the IXL skill
    is assigned. If the skill comes off the list, these five questions come out with it.)
11. **Graphing a line has two methods.** From y = mx + b: start at the y-intercept, move with the slope, and take a third
    point as the check. From anything else: Unit 11's table of values. A negative slope "goes down as you move right": −2/3
    is down 2, right 3.
12. **Whiteboards read; the Independent Set draws** (Unit 11's and Unit 7's decision). The six-question handout prints
    empty grids on 10.05 and 10.06, and **Your Turn 1 of each is the handout's question 1, drawn in class** with the teacher
    circulating. A question that asks for a drawn line uses continuous quantities (time, distance, weight), so a line is
    right and not a row of dots.
13. **MA.8.AR.3.5 is one line at a time.** The state: "it is not the expectation to compare slopes or y-intercepts of two
    linear equations". No question compares two lines' slopes. An interpretation is a sentence with both units: "The
    barrel gains 4.5 gallons every hour."; "The barrel held 12 gallons when the rain began."
14. **Temperature** (the benchmark's clarification): F = 1.8C + 32 is given whenever it is used and never memorized; it is
    taught in 10.07+08 as "linear, not proportional". **Lines of fit** (the same clarification) get a preview only — one
    whiteboard question in 10.10 that explains itself, with no new word and no picture — because Unit 13 teaches scatter
    plots and lines of fit (13.06–13.09); none is on this test.
15. **"Linear function"** is IXL's name for a linear relationship (its skills from 10.04 on use it). It is said once, in a
    line of 10.04's Notes I; the pages keep "linear relationship", and Unit 12 defines "function".
16. **Letters.** m and b are the slope and the y-intercept in y = mx + b and nothing else: a quantity is never named m or
    b, and never o, i or l. Multiplication is a dot or parentheses, never ×. Money has two decimals when it has cents; a
    slope may be a fraction or a decimal, and both are accepted where both are exact.
17. **Calculators are allowed and never mentioned** (ruling of 2 Sep). The Reference Sheet is not used on the test.

**MA.8.AR.3.1 — linear, and also proportional? (3)**
1. A table of a linear relationship: x 2, 4, 6, 8; y 7, 13, 19, 25. *What is y when x is 0? Is the relationship also
   proportional?* One question with two answers, and one point for both: 1; no.
2. *Each equation or description shows a linear relationship between x and y. Select all that are also proportional.*
   (the stem never says how many; one point for exactly A, B, D): A y = 3.5x ✓ · B y = x ✓ (the constant is 1) ·
   C y = 6 + 2x (the number added is written first) · D y = −(1/2)x ✓ (a negative constant) · E y is the cost, in
   dollars, of renting a van for x miles: \$40 plus \$0.30 for every mile (a start amount).
3. *Which graph shows a proportional relationship?* Four graphs, each on its own axes, the same scales on all four:
   A the points (2, 3), (4, 6), (6, 9), with no point at the origin ✓ (they lie on a line that reaches (0, 0)) · B a
   line through (0, 2) and (4, 6) (straight, not through the origin) · C a curve through (0, 0), (2, 2) and (4, 8)
   (through the origin, not straight) · D the points (1, 4), (2, 6), (3, 8), with no line drawn (a constant rate of
   change taken for a constant ratio: y ÷ x is 4, then 3). Two graphs are dots and two are drawn, so the choice
   cannot be made by which is which.

**MA.8.AR.3.2 — slope (5)**
4. *A line passes through (−2, 7) and (4, −5). Find its slope.* −2.
5. A table of a linear relationship: x −6, −2, 2, 10; y −7, −2, 3, 13. *Find the slope.* 5/4 (1.25).
6. A graph: the water in a tank, in gallons, versus time, in minutes; the gallons axis counts by 40; the line starts at
   (0, 240) and the points (2, 160) and (5, 40) are marked. (a) *Find the slope of the line.* −40. (b) *What does the
   slope tell you?* how many gallons the tank loses every minute ✓ · how many gallons are added to the tank every minute
   (the sign ignored) · how many gallons the tank held at the start (the y-intercept) · how many minutes the tank takes to lose
   1 gallon (the ratio turned over). The four choices carry no number, so (b) does not give (a) away.
7. A line through (0, 1) on a grid that runs to 10, with one slope triangle drawn on it: a horizontal side of 3 and a
   vertical side of 2. *A larger slope triangle on the same line has a horizontal side of 15. How long is its vertical
   side?* 10. The larger triangle is past the edge of the grid, so the answer cannot be counted off the picture.

**MA.8.AR.3.4 — graph a line (3)**
8. *Graph y = −(3/4)x + 2 on the grid* (−6 to 6 on both axes). One point: a straight line through (0, 2) and (4, −1)
   that runs on past both points; arrows are not required. The key prints the graph.
9. A candle is 12 inches tall. It burns down 1.5 inches every hour. (a) *Complete the table* — hours 0, 2, 4, 6; height
   in inches ▢, ▢, ▢, ▢ (12, 9, 6, 3; one point for all four). (b) *Graph the candle's height versus time on the grid* —
   hours 0 to 8, inches 0 to 12, both counting by 1. One point: a straight line that starts at (0, 12) and passes
   through (2, 9), (4, 6) and (6, 3); it may end anywhere from (6, 3) to (8, 0). Points with no line through them do
   not earn the point (time is continuous). The key prints the graph.

**MA.8.AR.3.5 — what the slope and the y-intercept mean (5)**
10. c = 1.75t + 9.50 gives the cost c, in dollars, of a pizza with t toppings. *Match each number in the equation with
    its meaning* — a matching grid, as Unit 11's paper has: the rows 1.75 and 9.50; the columns A the cost of a pizza
    with no toppings · B the amount each topping adds to the cost · C the number of toppings on the pizza · D the cost
    of a pizza with one topping. One point a row: 1.75 is B; 9.50 is A. (D, \$11.25, is neither number.)
11. A tree grows at a constant rate. Years after planting 2, 4, 7; height in feet 9, 14, 21.5. (a) *Find the slope, in
    feet per year.* 2.5. (b) *How tall was the tree when it was planted?* 4 feet — the y-intercept, asked as the
    story asks it.
12. F = 1.8C + 32 gives the temperature F, in degrees Fahrenheit, for a temperature of C degrees Celsius. *What does the
    slope, 1.8, mean?* For every increase of 1 degree Celsius, the Fahrenheit temperature increases 1.8 degrees ✓ · The
    Fahrenheit temperature is 1.8 times the Celsius temperature (read as proportional) · A temperature of 0 degrees
    Celsius is 1.8 degrees Fahrenheit (the slope read as the y-intercept) · For every increase of 1 degree Fahrenheit, the
    Celsius temperature increases 1.8 degrees (turned over).

**MA.8.AR.3.3 — write the equation (4)**
13. A graph: a line with the points (0, −4) and (3, 1) marked. *Write the equation of the line in slope-intercept form.*

14. A table of a linear relationship: x 2, 3, 5, 8; y 6, 2, −6, −18. *Write its equation in slope-intercept form.*

## 10.01 · Fri 15 Jan · Linear and Proportional Relationships · MA.8.AR.3.1

IXL: Find the constant of proportionality from a table — ZCK; Identify proportional relationships by graphing — RXD;
Identify proportional relationships — Z64; Write equations for proportional relationships from tables and graphs — VXM;
Interpret graphs of proportional relationships — Q96.

**Target.** I can decide whether a linear relationship shown in a table, a graph, an equation or a description is also
proportional, and explain how I know.

**Warm-up.** (the last class, 7.07) Car A travels 150 miles in 3 hours. Car B travels 110 miles in 2 hours. Which car
is faster? Car B (55 miles per hour; Car A travels 50) · (last week, 7.04) A table has the x-values 2, 4, 6 and the
y-values 5, 10, 15. Is y proportional to x? Yes; y ÷ x is 2.5 for every pair · (the unit before, 11.07) For y = 2x + 3,
find y when x is 0, 1 and 2. 3, 5, 7 · (the prerequisite) Find 15 ÷ 6 and 7 − 2(2.5). 2.5 and 2.

**Vocabulary.** rate of change — the amount y changes for every 1 unit that x increases · linear relationship — a
relationship with a constant rate of change; its graph is a straight line · proportional relationship — a linear
relationship in which y is 0 when x is 0: y = px (Unit 7's word, placed inside the new one).

**Notes.** I — *Linear: a constant rate of change.* x 0, 1, 2, 3; y 3, 5, 7, 9: y increases 2 for every 1 unit of x.
The same relationship with x counting by 2 — x 0, 2, 4, 6; y 3, 7, 11, 15: y increases 4 for every 2 units of x, which
is 2 for every 1 unit. Its graph is a straight line (Unit 11 drew it: y = 2x + 3). II — *Also proportional? Look at
x = 0.* One grid, two lines: y = 2x and y = 2x + 3. Both have the rate of change 2. y = 2x passes through the origin:
proportional. y = 2x + 3 passes through (0, 3): linear, not proportional. Beside them, a small third picture: a curve
through the origin — not proportional, because a proportional relationship is linear first. III — *When the table has no row for x = 0.*
x 2, 4, 6; y 9, 13, 17: the rate of change is 4 for every 2 units, which is 2 for every 1 unit; go back 2 units from
x = 2: 9 − 2 − 2 = 5, so y is 5 when x is 0: not proportional. x 4, 8, 12; y 2, 4, 6: the rate of change is 2 for
every 4 units, which is 0.5 for every 1 unit; go back 4 units from x = 4: 2 − 4(0.5) = 0: proportional — and y ÷ x is
0.5 for every pair, Unit 7's test.

**Examples.** 1 — a table with no row for x = 0: x 4, 8, 12; y 9, 15, 21. The rate of change is 6 for every 4 units,
1.5 for every 1 unit; 9 − 4(1.5) = 3, so y is 3 when x is 0: linear, not proportional. *Your Turn:* x 5, 10, 15;
y 6, 12, 18: the rate of change is 1.2; 6 − 5(1.2) = 0: proportional (the answer differs from the Example's on
purpose). 2 — two descriptions: a boat rental costs \$12 for every hour plus a \$20 deposit; a bike rental costs \$9
for every hour. Write an equation for each cost c, in dollars, after h hours; which relationship is also proportional?
c = 12h + 20 and c = 9h: the bike's, because 0 hours cost \$0; the boat costs \$20 at 0 hours. *Your Turn:* one plant
is 6 centimeters tall and grows 1.5 centimeters every week; a second plant sprouts today and grows 2 centimeters every
week. h = 1.5w + 6 and h = 2w: the second plant's.

**Boards.**
1. A table: x 0, 1, 2, 3; y 4, 7, 10, 13. The table shows a linear relationship. What is its rate of change? → 3.
2. The same table. Is the relationship also proportional? How do you know? → No: y is 4 when x is 0 (one thing changes:
   the question).
3. *MC* A table: x 4, 8, 12, 16; y 3, 6, 9, 12. The table shows a linear relationship. Is it also proportional? Yes: y ÷ x
   is 0.75 for every pair ✓ · No: the table has no row for x = 0 (the state's named misconception) · No: y is 3 when x is
   0 (the first row read as the start) · No: y is not a whole-number multiple of x (the constant thought to be whole).
4. y = 6x − 1 is a linear relationship. Is it also proportional? How do you know? → No: y is −1 when x is 0.
5. *MC, four graphs in a row* Which graph shows a proportional relationship? A the points (1, 3), (2, 6), (3, 9), with
   no point at the origin ✓ · B a line through (0, 3) and (3, 6) (straight, not through the origin) · C a curve through
   (0, 0), (1, 1) and (2, 4) (through the origin, not straight) · D the points (2, 5), (4, 6), (6, 7), with no line
   drawn (a constant rate of change taken for a constant ratio).
6. *Select all* the equations that show a proportional relationship: A y = 2 + 5x (a number added, written first) ·
   B y = (2/5)x ✓ · C y = 5(x + 2) (distribute: 5x + 10) · D y = −5x ✓ · E y = x/5 ✓ (the same as (1/5)x).
7. *Extra number* A taxi with 4 seats charges \$3.50 to start and \$2.25 for every mile. Is the cost proportional to
   the number of miles? Explain. → No: a ride of 0 miles costs \$3.50.
8. *Find the start* A linear relationship includes the pairs (2, 11) and (5, 23). What is y when x is 0? → 3 (the rate
   of change is 4).
9. *Written* Mia says the table with the x-values 1, 2, 3 and the y-values 6, 8, 10 shows a proportional relationship,
   because y increases by the same amount every time. Is Mia correct? Explain. — No: a constant increase makes the
   relationship linear; y is 4 when x is 0 (or: y ÷ x is 6, then 4), so it is not proportional.

**Independent set (kinds).** a table that shows x = 0: decide, with the reason · a table with no row for x = 0, x
counting by 3: find y when x is 0, then decide · a graph of three points with no point at the origin: decide · *select
