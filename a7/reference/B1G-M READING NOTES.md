# B1G-M reading notes — Grade 7 and Grade 8 (FDOE B.E.S.T. Instructional Guide for Mathematics)

Source: two zips Croix uploaded 20 Sept 2026, extracted to text + page images.
Grade 7: 114 pages. Grade 8: 128 pages. Read cover to cover; these notes are the durable record.
Purpose: backbone for the fresh A7 build. Per benchmark I record what the guide says we must
cover (benchmark + clarifications), what it warns about (misconceptions), and the items it gives
(so our question banks can be checked against state-shaped items).

## Guide structure (both grades identical)
Each benchmark page carries: Benchmark + Clarifications · Connecting Benchmarks (horizontal) ·
Glossary terms · Vertical Alignment (previous / next) · Purpose and Instructional Strategies ·
Common Misconceptions or Errors · Strategies to Support Tiered Instruction · Instructional Tasks
(MTR-tagged) · Instructional Items. Footer on every benchmark: "examples… not comprehensive."

## MTRs (front matter, both guides)
MTR.1.1 actively participate in effortful learning · MTR.2.1 represent problems in multiple ways ·
MTR.3.1 fluency (procedural + conceptual) · MTR.4.1 engage in discussion (partner talk — NOT ours,
settled rule: no partner/group work; the guide's Part-B "compare with a partner" tasks get dropped
or rewritten as "compare against the given expression") · MTR.5.1 use patterns and structure ·
MTR.6.1 assess reasonableness · MTR.7.1 apply to real world.

## Grade 7 Areas of Emphasis
1. Fractions, decimals, percents as rational numbers; fluency in the four operations.
2. Equivalent expressions; solve equations and inequalities.
3. Proportional relationships in two variables.
4. 2D/3D figures incl. circles and cylinders.
5. Categorical and numerical data; probability.

# GRADE 7

## MA.7.NSO.1.1 — Laws of Exponents (rational bases, whole-number exponents)
Misconception: squaring means ×2. Instruction avoids "power" vs "exponent" confusion.

## MA.7.NSO.1.2 — Rewrite rational numbers in equivalent forms (fractions, decimals incl. repeating, percents)
Misconception: truncating instead of rounding; treating repeating decimals as terminating. Item shape:
Justin's 17/3 example (which representation is exact).

## MA.7.NSO.2.1 — Order of operations with rational numbers
Clarification: up to 6 steps. Guide explicitly discourages PEMDAS-as-mnemonic (left-to-right of
same-rank operations). Misconception: multiply before divide regardless of order.

## MA.7.NSO.2.2 — Four operations with rational numbers, procedural fluency
Includes complex fractions. Same-type rule: fractions given → fractions answered.

## MA.7.NSO.2.3 — Four operations with rational numbers in real-world contexts
Misconceptions: applying the most recently taught operation; "circle the numbers / underline the
question" keyword-hunting that strips context. Tiered: three-read strategy; estimate first.
Items: recycling prize $800 split by 237/192/179 lb (proportional share); Fun Warehouse menu
(Go-Karts 9.75, Laser Tag 7.50, Inflatables 5.00, Mini-Bowling 2.25, Video Games 0.75; $3 entry;
$20 each). Item 1 answer: 20 − 3 − 7.50 − 2.25 − 4(0.75) = 4.25 each.
Task 3: cupcakes — 2 c flour, 1/2 c butter, 3/4 c sugar per batch; has 8 c flour, 2 c butter,
2 c sugar → flour 4 batches, butter 4, sugar 2⅔ → 2 batches; one more batch needs ¼ c sugar.

## MA.7.AR.1.1 — Add and subtract linear expressions with rational coefficients
Clarification 1: forms ax ± b or b ± ax, a and b rational. Example (from page image): (7x − 4) − (2 − ½x) = 7½x − 6 = 15/2 x − 6. Guide: avoid variables o, i, l. Same-type rule again.
Misconceptions: combining unlike terms; dropping the distributed negative: 3 − (2x + 7) → 3 − 2x + 7.
Tiered: write −(…) as +(−1)(…). Items: (5x − 1) − (0.06x − 4 + 0.4x) = 4.54x + 3;
(4 + ⅔x) + (⅙x − 3) = ⅚x + 1; (2x − ⅘ − 3x) − (−8x + 2) = 7x − 2⅘ = 7x − 14/5.
Vertical: next MA.8.AR.1.2, MA.8.AR.1.3.

## MA.7.AR.1.2 — Determine whether two linear expressions are equivalent
Any form, rational coefficients. Guide: give multiple equivalent forms, "not just the most
simplified"; ½x ≡ ⅕x + 3/10 x. Item 1 is a matching item (verified on the page image and worked):
(3½x − 4 1/10) − (⅜x + 2⅕) = 3⅛x − 6 3/10; (3½x − 4 1/10) + (⅜x + 2⅕) = 3⅞x − 1 9/10;
2(1½x − 1 3/10) + ½(1⅖ + ¼x) = 3⅛x − 1 9/10; 2(1½x − 1 3/10) − (1⅖ + ¼x) = 2¾x − 4.
Task 1 Part A source expression: 4/3(6 − x) + 3(⅔x + 1) = ⅔x + 11.
Vertical: next MA.8.AR.1.1, MA.8.AR.1.3.

## MA.7.AR.2.1 — Write and solve one-step inequalities; represent solutions algebraically or graphically
Forms px > q, x/p > q, x ± p > q, p ± x > q; any symbol; variable either side.
Must show WHY the symbol flips: 6 > −7 times −3 gives −18 > 21, false. Misconceptions: open vs
closed circle; symbol direction = shading direction; division property of equality vs inequality.
Vertical: next MA.8.AR.2.2 (two-step inequalities), MA.8.GR.1.3.
Tiered: test x = 2 in x + 3 > 5 → 5 > 5 false, so 2 is not a solution (this is the shape for a
"which value is a solution" distractor). Task 1 error-analysis: (A) 3b ≥ 120 → b ≤ 40 is WRONG
(should be b ≥ 40); (B) 2.55 < 4.2 − x → x < −1.65 is WRONG (x < 1.65); (C) p + 8 < 25 → p < 17
correct. Task 2: "using integers −5..5 no more than once, finish  □x + □ ≤ □ so solution is x ≥ ½"
(needs negative coefficient — flip). Items: 12 < a/4 (a > 48); 4.2 + z ≤ −5.3 (z ≤ −9.5);
−0.125c > 0.375 (c < −3).

## MA.7.AR.2.2 — Write and solve two-step equations, all terms rational
Forms px ± q = r and p(x ± q) = r; variable either side; real-world or mathematical.
Guide: do NOT force one order — 4(x + 7) = 12 may be divided by 4 or distributed; compare
efficiency. Students should name the property at each step (Appendix D). Models: algebra tiles,
bar diagrams (2x − 10 = −26), balances (3x + 4 = −11).
Misconceptions: applying add/subtract on the SAME side; misidentifying constant vs coefficient in
context (Cameron's tank: 12 gal capacity, 2.5 gal/min, 3.4 gal already → 2.5m + 3.4 = 12, m = 3.44).
Tasks: plumber — materials 341.25, total 424.09, $20.71/h → 20.71h + 341.25 = 424.09, h = 4;
rectangle L = 2W, P = 45 → 6W = 45, W = 7.5 ft.
Items: 7/9 = ⅔x − 7 → x = 35/3 = 11⅔ ("exact value" — fraction, not decimal);
5.6(3z − 2) = 11 → 3z − 2 = 1.9642857… → z ≈ 1.3214 (= 37/28 exactly). Note: state item gives
an ugly answer; "exact" wording matters.
Vertical: next MA.8.AR.2.1 (multi-step), MA.8.AR.2.3.

## MA.7.DP.1.1 — Appropriate measure of center or variation, considering context and outliers
Clar. 2: graphs limited to histograms, line plots, box plots, stem-and-leaf. Clar. 3: center =
mean, median ONLY; variation = range, IQR ONLY. Rule of thumb from the guide: ignore outliers
("typical" members, politics/advertising) → median + IQR; keep outliers (risk, insurance,
medical) → mean + range. Outliers identified QUALITATIVELY; the quantitative 1.5·IQR fence is
teacher-optional (example five-number summary 29, 37, 43, 49, 82 → IQR 12, fences 19 and 67 →
82 is an outlier). Misconceptions: miscalculating; assuming symmetry; misidentifying outliers.
Task: Electoral College table 2012/2016/2020 (page 92–93; table on image) — most votes
(California 55), second (Texas 38), shape (skewed right), outliers, which center (median),
median value.
Items: 15 household incomes (32,47,47,36,35,48,35,32,48,34,50,36,42,35,42 thousands) — no
outlier → mean $39,933 (sum 599/15) or median $36,000; guide wants the JUSTIFICATION. Add a
$475,000 family (16 values) → median: sorted 8th = 36, 9th = 42 → $39,000; mean would jump to
$67,125, which is the reason median is the appropriate choice.
Note for our items: state item shape is "choose the measure AND compute it AND justify."
Vertical: next MA.8.DP.1.1, HS DP.1.1.
