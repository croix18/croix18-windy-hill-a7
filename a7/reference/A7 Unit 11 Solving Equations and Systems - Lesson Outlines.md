# A7 Unit 11 — Solving Equations and Systems — Lesson Outlines

*Written 14 November 2026 from the benchmarks first (`Florida BEST Grade 8 - Source of Truth.md` §MA.8.AR.2.1 and
MA.8.AR.4.1–4.3, the B1G-M reading notes, the FAST item shapes), then read against Math Nation 11.1–11.8 (the unit
audit). Seven teaching days, Mon 30 Nov – Thu 10 Dec 2026, and the one-period test Fri 11 Dec (rulings 49, 51). Every
equation below was solved by sympy before it was written down (126 of them, 14 Nov); the specs re-derive them again at
build time. HOUSE STYLE §16 and ruling 43: the warm-up, the notes and the worked examples are what the class has been
shown, and every practice number after them is new.*

## The unit's standing decisions (each reversible)

1. **Unit 11 comes before Unit 10 and Unit 7** (ruling 52: its equations are the district's Q2 essential benchmark).
   So the class has never graphed y = mx + b and has no word for slope. **A line is graphed from a table of values**
   (x = 0 and two more; the third point is the check), and every system is given already solved for y — MA.8.AR.4.3's
   boundary is slope-intercept form only, so nothing needs rearranging. Unit 10 names the slope in January.
2. **"Slope" and "y-intercept" are not on a student page in this unit.** Parallel lines are "the same steepness: the
   same number multiplying x" (AR.4.3 Clar. 3 asks the class to recognise it; Unit 10 gives it its name). The Teacher
   Edition may say slope.
3. **No substitution and no elimination** (Florida: Algebra 1). One bridge is taught and not tested: at the crossing
   point both equations give the same y, so the x of the crossing solves *mx + b = nx + c* — the equation of 11.02.
   It is how a graphed answer is checked, and it is why equations and systems share a unit.
4. **Three endings, named every day from 11.03 on**: x = a (one solution), a = a (true for every number: infinitely
   many), a = b (never true: no solution). x = 0 is one solution, and a board says so (the guide's named
   misconception: "a solution must look like variable = constant").
5. **Rational coefficients carry 11.04**: a fraction distributed, both sides multiplied by a common denominator, and
   a decimal distributed — the last is the lowest-scoring released Grade 8 skill (21%), so it has a board every day
   from 11.01 and its own question on the test.
6. **Every solution is checked by substitution** — in an equation, and in both equations of a system (AR.4.1: a
   solution satisfies both).
7. **Approximate solutions (AR.4.3 Clar. 1)** are read between the grid lines to the nearest half or tenth and
   written with "about"; an item accepts the estimate a careful reader makes and names the swapped pair and the
   nearest grid point as its errors.
8. **Variables rotate** (x, y for systems; n, w, m, h, v elsewhere; never o, i, l). Multiplication is written with
   parentheses or a dot, never × (Croix, Nov 2026).
9. **Whiteboards read a graph or a table; the Independent Set is where a student draws.** A blank whiteboard is no
   place for two lines, so boards show the graph (or two tables) and ask for the solution, the count or the meaning;
   the six-question handout prints empty grids for 11.07 and 11.08.

## 11.01 · Mon 30 Nov · Equations with Like Terms and the Distributive Property · MA.8.AR.2.1

IXL: Solve equations involving like terms — Q2B; Solve equations using the distributive property — QWE; Solve
two-step equations: word problems — 8CZ (due by the Unit 11 test).

**Target.** I can solve an equation that has like terms to collect or parentheses to distribute.

**Warm-up.** (last lesson, T-B2) a triangle's angles are 47° and 68°: the third is 65° · (last unit, 6.07) similar
triangles, 6/9 = x/12: x = 8 · (Unit 5) 3 cm on a 1 cm : 20 km map: 60 km · (the prerequisite, 1.03) 3x + 5 = 26: x = 7.

**Notes.** I — *Like terms are collected first*: 3x + 5x − 4 = 28 → 8x − 4 = 28 → x = 4; what a like term is (same
letter, same power); the equation is then the two-step equation of Unit 1. II — *Distribute, then collect*:
3(2x − 5) + 4 = 25 → 6x − 15 + 4 = 25 → 6x − 11 = 25 → x = 6; the multiplier goes to both terms inside.
III — *Subtracting a group*: 18 − 3(x − 2) = 9 → 18 − 3x + 6 = 9 → 24 − 3x = 9 → x = 5; the −3 is distributed, so
−3 · −2 = +6. Check: 18 − 3(3) = 9.

**Examples.** 1 — 5x + 9 − 2x = 30: 3x + 9 = 30, x = 7. *Your Turn:* 7x − 3 − 4x = 24: x = 9.
2 — 5(x − 3) + 2x = 41: 7x − 15 = 41, x = 8. *Your Turn:* 4(x − 2) + 3x = 34: x = 6.

**Boards.**
1. 2x + 6x = 56 → x = 7.
2. 9y − 4y + 3 = 48 → y = 9.
3. 3(n + 4) + 2n = 62 → n = 10.
4. *MC* 2(2x − 5) + x = 20: x = 6 ✓ · 5 (distributed to the first term only) · 2 (−10 became +10) · 7.5 (the + x lost).
5. 15 − 2(w + 1) = 5 → w = 4.
6. *MC* 12 − 3(x − 4) = 6: the next line is 24 − 3x = 6 ✓ · −3x = 6 (−3 · −4 taken as −12) · 9(x − 4) = 6
   (subtracted before distributing) · 8 − 3x = 6 (the −3 never reached the 4).
7. *Extra number* A gym charges \$25 to join and \$12 a month. Dana, who is 13, has paid \$97. How many months?
   12m + 25 = 97, m = 6.
8. *Backwards* x = 8 is the solution of 3(x + ▢) + 2x = 46: the box is 2.
9. *Written* Sam solved 4(x − 2) + 3 = 23 as 4x − 2 + 3 = 23, x = 5.5. Find the mistake and the solution: the 4
   never reached the 2; 4x − 8 + 3 = 23, x = 7.

**Independent set (kinds).** collect like terms · distribute, then two steps · subtract a group · a decimal
distributed, 0.5(4x + 6) + x = 18 (x = 5) · a join-fee story · which line has the mistake.

## 11.02 · Tue 1 Dec · Equations with Variables on Both Sides · MA.8.AR.2.1

IXL: Solve equations with variables on both sides — ZYL.

**Target.** I can solve an equation that has the variable on both sides, and check the solution in both sides.

**Warm-up.** (yesterday) 3(x + 2) + x = 26: x = 5 · (last week, T-B2) an exterior angle with remote angles 50° and
65°: 115° · (last unit, 6.03) hypotenuse 13, leg 5: the other leg is 12 · (the prerequisite) simplify 2x − 5x: −3x.

**Notes.** I — *Take the same x-term from both sides*: 5x + 2 = 3x + 10 → 2x + 2 = 10 → x = 4; check, 22 = 22.
The balance stays level only if both sides lose the same thing. II — *Either side works*: 2x + 9 = 6x − 11 → take 2x:
9 = 4x − 11, x = 5; take 6x instead: −4x + 9 = −11, x = 5. Moving the smaller x-term keeps the coefficient positive.
III — *Simplify each side first*: 3(x + 4) = 5x − 2 → 3x + 12 = 5x − 2 → 14 = 2x → x = 7.

**Examples.** 1 — 7x − 5 = 4x + 13: 3x = 18, x = 6. *Your Turn:* 8x − 9 = 5x + 15: x = 8.
2 — 2(3x − 1) = 4x + 9: 2x = 11, x = 5.5 (a solution need not be whole). *Your Turn:* 4(2x − 3) = 6x + 1: x = 6.5.

**Boards.**
1. 6x = 2x + 28 → x = 7.
2. 9x + 4 = 5x + 40 → x = 9.
3. 3n − 8 = 7n + 12 → n = −5.
4. *MC* 5x + 8 = x − 16: x = −6 ✓ · −4 (added the x-terms: 6x = −24) · −2 (added 8 instead of taking it away) ·
   6 (the sign lost at the last step).
5. 2(x + 7) = 5x − 4 → x = 6.
6. *MC* 4(x − 2) = 2(x + 5): x = 9 ✓ · 3.5 (each multiplier reached only the first term) · 6.5 (the right side not
   distributed to the 5) · 3 (the x-terms added: 6x = 18).
7. *Extra number* A gym open 7 days a week has two plans: \$30 a month plus \$5 a visit, or \$8 a visit. After how
   many visits in a month do they cost the same? 30 + 5v = 8v, v = 10.
8. *Backwards* x = 3 is the solution of 5x + ▢ = 2x + 16: the box is 7.
9. *Written* Kai starts 2x + 15 = 6x − 9 by taking 2x from both sides; Lena takes 6x. Solve it both ways: x = 6
   both times — the same step done to both sides keeps the equation true whichever term goes.

**Independent set (kinds).** both sides, whole solution · a negative solution · parentheses on one side ·
parentheses on both · a solution that is a decimal · two plans that cost the same.

## 11.03 · Wed 2 Dec (43 min) · One Solution, No Solution, Infinitely Many Solutions · MA.8.AR.2.1

IXL: Find the number of solutions — XDE; Create equations with no solutions or infinitely many solutions — 7TY.

**Target.** I can tell whether an equation has one solution, no solution or infinitely many solutions.

**Warm-up.** (yesterday) 6x − 4 = 2x + 20: x = 6 · (this week, 11.01) 2(x + 5) + 3x = 30: x = 4 · (last unit,
6.05) the distance from (1, 2) to (4, 6): 5 · (the prerequisite) is x = 2 a solution of 3x + 1 = 7? Yes.

**Notes.** I — *Three endings*: 2x + 5 = 3x + 9 ends x = −4, one solution; 2x + 5 = 2x + 9 ends 5 = 9, never true,
no solution; 2x + 5 = 2x + 5 ends 5 = 5, always true, infinitely many solutions (every number works). II — *See it
before the end*: simplify each side; different x-terms → one solution; same x-terms, different constants → none;
same x-terms, same constants → infinitely many. 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6 (infinitely many); 3(x + 2) = 3x + 2 (none).
III — *Zero is a number*: 4x + 7 = 7 − 2x → 6x = 0 → x = 0: one solution, not "no solution".

**Examples.** 1 — 4(x − 3) + 2 = 4x − 10: 4x − 10 = 4x − 10, infinitely many. *Your Turn:* 3(2x + 1) − 5 = 6x − 2:
infinitely many. 2 — 2(3x + 4) = 6x + 5: 8 = 5, no solution. *Your Turn:* 5(2x − 1) = 10x + 3: no solution.

**Boards.**
1. 7x + 2 = 7x + 2 → infinitely many.
2. 3x + 8 = 3x − 1 → no solution.
3. 5x + 4 = 2x + 4 → one solution, x = 0.
4. *MC* 2(4x + 3) = 8x + 6: infinitely many ✓ · no solution (the x-terms cancelled, so "nothing is left") · one
   solution, x = 0 (read 0 = 0 as x = 0) · one solution, x = 6 (the constant taken for the answer).
5. 6x − 9 = 3(2x − 4) → −9 = −12, no solution.
6. *MC* Which equation has no solution? 3x + 1 = 3x − 1 ✓ · 3x + 1 = x + 1 (x = 0 is a solution) · 3x + 1 = 1 + 3x
   (the same on both sides: infinitely many) · 3x + 1 = 2x (x = −1).
7. *Extra number* Two plans with an 8 GB limit: \$15 plus \$3 a gigabyte, or \$20 plus \$3 a gigabyte. For how many
   gigabytes do they cost the same? 15 + 3g = 20 + 3g: never — no solution.
8. *Backwards* Fill the box so 4(x + 3) = 4x + ▢ has infinitely many solutions (12); then so 2(3x − 5) = ▢x + 1
   has no solution (6).
9. *Written* Mia ended at 0 = 0 and wrote "x = 0". Jo ended at 0 = 0 and wrote "no solution". Who is right for
   3(x + 4) − 12 = 3x? Neither: 3x = 3x is true for every number — infinitely many.

**Independent set (kinds).** one solution · no solution · infinitely many · the x = 0 case · fill the box:
infinitely many · fill the box: none.

## 11.04 · Fri 4 Dec · Multi-Step Equations · MA.8.AR.2.1

IXL: Solve multi-step equations — 55K; Solve multi-step equations: complete the solution — PGH; Solve equations:
mixed review — HZZ.

**Target.** I can solve a multi-step equation with fraction or decimal coefficients and check it.

**Warm-up.** (last lesson) how many solutions has 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6? Infinitely many · (this week, 11.02)
9x − 7 = 4x + 18: x = 5 · (last unit, 6.01) two sides are 5 and 9: the third is between 4 and 14 · (the
prerequisite) ⅔ of 9: 6.

**Notes.** I — *One order for every equation*: distribute; collect like terms on each side; x-terms to one side;
constants to the other; divide; check. 3(2x − 4) + 5 = 2(x + 6) + 1 → 6x − 7 = 2x + 13 → x = 5. II — *Fractions*:
distribute the fraction, ⅓(6x + 9) = x + 7 → 2x + 3 = x + 7 → x = 4; or multiply both sides by a common
denominator, x/2 + 3 = x/3 + 5 → 3x + 18 = 2x + 30 → x = 12. III — *Decimals*: 0.5(8x − 6) = 1.5x + 2 →
4x − 3 = 1.5x + 2 → 2.5x = 5 → x = 2; the decimal multiplies both terms inside.

**Examples.** 1 — 2(3x + 1) − 4x = 3(x − 2) + 1: 2x + 2 = 3x − 5, x = 7. *Your Turn:* 4(x + 2) − x = 2(x − 3) + 20:
x = 6. 2 — ¾(8x − 4) = 2x + 9: 6x − 3 = 2x + 9, x = 3. *Your Turn:* ⅔(9x − 6) = 2x + 12: x = 4.

**Boards.**
1. 5(x − 2) + 3 = 2x + 11 → x = 6.
2. ½(10x + 8) = 3x + 14 → x = 5.
3. 0.4(5x − 10) = x + 3 → x = 7.
4. *MC* −2(3x − 5) = 4x − 30: x = 4 ✓ · 2 (−2 · −5 taken as −10) · 2.5 (the −2 never reached the 5) · −20 (4x
   taken from the right but −6x left where it was).
5. x/3 − 1 = x/5 + 1 → x = 15.
6. *MC* How many solutions has ½(6x − 4) = 3(x − 1) + 1? Infinitely many ✓ · none · one, x = 0 · one, x = 2.
7. *Extra number* Shop A rents a bike for \$12 plus \$4.50 an hour. Shop B, 3 blocks away, charges \$7.50 an hour.
   For how many hours is the cost the same? 12 + 4.5h = 7.5h, h = 4.
8. *Backwards* x = 2 is the solution of 3(x + ▢) = 2x + 20: the box is 6.
9. *Written* Solve 1.5(4x − 2) + x = 2(x + 6) and check: x = 3; both sides are 18.

**Independent set (kinds).** whole-number multi-step · a fraction distributed · a common denominator · a decimal
distributed · how many solutions (fraction coefficients) · a rental story.

## 11.05–06 · Mon 7 Dec · Systems: Which Points Are Solutions; How Many Solutions · MA.8.AR.4.1 (with MA.8.AR.4.2)

IXL: Is (x, y) a solution to the system of equations? — N46; Find the number of solutions to a system of equations
by graphing — AGZ; Solve a system of equations in slope-intercept form — 9Z8.

**Target.** I can decide whether an ordered pair is a solution of a system, and read from a graph how many solutions
a system has.

**Warm-up.** (last lesson) ½(4x + 6) = x + 8: x = 5 · (last week, 11.03) how many solutions has 3x + 4 = 3x − 4?
None · (last unit, T-B1) vertical angles: one is 72°, so is the other · (the prerequisite) y = 2x + 1: find y when
x = 3: 7.

**Notes.** I — *One equation in x and y has many solutions, and they make a line*: y = x + 2 with its table (0, 2),
(1, 3), (2, 4) and the line through them; (1, 3) is a solution because 3 = 1 + 2; every point of the line is one.
II — *A system is two equations; its solution makes both true*: y = x + 2 and y = −2x + 8. (2, 4): 4 = 2 + 2 and
4 = −4 + 8 — a solution. (0, 2): true in the first, 2 ≠ 8 in the second — not a solution. On the graph it is the
point both lines pass through. III — *How many solutions, from the graph*: the lines cross once (one solution);
they are parallel and never meet (no solution); they are the same line (infinitely many — every point of it).
Three small graphs: y = x + 1 and y = −x + 5; y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x − 3; y = ½x + 1 and y = 0.5x + 1.

**Examples.** 1 — y = 3x − 1 and y = x + 5: which of (3, 8), (1, 2), (2, 7) is a solution? (3, 8); (1, 2) fails the
second, (2, 7) fails the first. *Your Turn:* y = 2x + 3 and y = −x + 9 with (2, 7), (0, 3), (4, 5): (2, 7).
2 — the graph of y = ½x + 2 and y = ½x − 1: how many solutions? None: parallel. *Your Turn:* the graph of
y = −x + 4 and y = 2x − 5: one solution, (3, 1).

**Boards.**
1. Is (4, 1) a solution of y = x − 3 and y = −x + 5? Yes: 1 = 1 and 1 = 1.
2. Is (2, 5) a solution of y = 2x + 1 and y = 3x − 2? No: 5 = 5, but 3(2) − 2 = 4.
3. *Graph* y = x + 5 and y = −2x − 1: how many solutions, and what? One, (−2, 3).
4. *MC* y = 2x − 4 and y = −x + 5: which pair is a solution? (3, 2) ✓ · (2, 0) (the first equation only) · (0, 5)
   (the second only) · (2, 3) (the coordinates swapped).
5. *Graph* two parallel lines: how many solutions? None.
6. *MC* Both equations of a system graph as the same line. How many solutions? Infinitely many ✓ · none (the lines
   never "cross") · one · two (the two points marked on the line).
7. *Extra number* Ana has y = 5x + 20 dollars and Ben has y = 10x dollars after x weeks of a 12-week term; the
   graph shows the lines crossing at (4, 40). What does that point mean? After 4 weeks each has \$40.
8. *Backwards* (2, ▢) is a solution of y = 3x + 1 and y = x + 5: the box is 7.
9. *Written* Leo says (1, 4) is a solution of y = 3x + 1 and y = 2x + 3 because it makes the first equation true.
   Is he right? No: 2(1) + 3 = 5, not 4. A solution makes both true; (2, 7) does.

**Independent set (kinds).** a pair that is a solution · a pair that fails one equation (which?) · select all the
solutions · a graph of parallel lines · a graph that crosses: the count and the point · one line for both.

## 11.07 · Wed 9 Dec (43 min) · Solving Systems by Graphing · MA.8.AR.4.3

2. *Two tables* y = 3x − 5 and y = x + 1 for x = 0 to 4: the solution? (3, 4).
3. *Graph* y = 2x + 4 and y = −x − 2: (−2, 0).
4. *MC, graph* y = −2x + 5 and y = x − 1: (2, 1) ✓ · (1, 2) (swapped) · (0, 5) (where one line meets the y-axis) ·
   (1, 0) (where the other meets the x-axis).
5. *Graph* y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x − 3: no solution — parallel.
6. *MC, graph* lines through (0, 1), (1, 3) and through (0, 4), (2, 2): which system? y = 2x + 1, y = −x + 4 ✓ ·
   y = x + 2, y = −x + 4 (fits the crossing point only) · y = 2x + 1, y = x + 4 (the falling line written rising) ·

7. *Extra number* Candle A, which cost \$3, is y = −2x + 12 cm tall after x hours; candle B is y = −x + 8. The
   graph shows both. When are they the same height? After 4 hours, at 4 cm.
8. *Backwards* (2, 5) is the solution of y = 2x + 1 and y = −x + ▢: the box is 7.
9. *Written* Tia graphed y = 3x − 2 and y = 3x + 1 and says the lines must cross somewhere off her grid. Is she

**Independent set (kinds; grids printed).** graph and solve, whole crossing · graph and solve, a negative
coordinate · a ½ multiplier · parallel: no solution · two tables to the solution · the same line twice.

## 11.08 · Thu 10 Dec · Systems in Context; Approximate Solutions · MA.8.AR.4.3

IXL: Solve a system of equations by graphing: word problems — W9J. (The Unit Review goes home today; ruling 27a.)

**Target.** I can solve a real-world system by graphing, say what the crossing point means, and estimate a
crossing that falls between the grid lines.

**Warm-up.** (yesterday) y = x + 3 and y = 2x from their tables: (3, 6) · (last week, 11.03) how many solutions
has 5x + 4 = 2x + 4? One, x = 0 · (last unit, 6.07) a 6-ft post casts a 4-ft shadow; a tree's is 10 ft: 15 ft ·
(the prerequisite) the number halfway between 2 and 3: 2.5.

**Notes.** I — *A story is a system*: each quantity is (the amount per 1)·x + (the start). A pass costs \$10 plus
\$2 a ride, y = 2x + 10; paying \$4 a ride is y = 4x. The lines cross at (5, 20): at 5 rides both cost \$20;
fewer rides, pay per ride; more, buy the pass. II — *Say what the point means, with units*: x is the number of
rides, y the cost; the axes carry the names and a scale. III — *Between the grid lines*: y = 2x − 1 and
y = −x + 4 cross between x = 1 and x = 2, a little above y = 2: about (1.7, 2.3). An estimate is checked by
putting it in both equations: 2.4 and 2.3, close.

**Examples.** 1 — Tank A holds 30 L and gains 5 L a minute; Tank B holds 60 L and gains 2 L a minute:
y = 5x + 30, y = 2x + 60, equal at 10 minutes, 80 L. *Your Turn:* Mia has \$40 and saves \$10 a week; Noah has
\$100 and saves \$5 a week: equal at 12 weeks, \$160. 2 — Printer A prints 3 pages a minute; Printer B has 5 pages
done and prints 1 a minute: y = 3x, y = x + 5, the crossing halfway between grid lines: (2.5, 7.5). *Your Turn:*
y = 4x and y = 2x + 3: (1.5, 6).

**Boards.**
1. *Graph, story* y = 3x + 6 and y = 5x: (3, 15).
2. *Graph, story* a crossing at (4, 28) on a cost-by-miles graph: what does it mean? At 4 miles both cost \$28.
3. *Graph* y = x and y = −2x + 5: between which two whole numbers is the x of the crossing? 1 and 2.
4. *MC, graph* y = 2x + 1 and y = −x + 5: closest to (1.3, 3.7) ✓ · (3.7, 1.3) (swapped) · (1, 3) (the nearest grid
   point, on one line only) · (0, 5) (where one line starts).
5. *Graph, story* y = 3x + 12 and y = 6x cross at (4, 24); which costs less for 7? The first: 33 against 42.
6. *MC* Gym A is \$20 to join and \$10 a month; Gym B is \$15 a month. Which system? y = 10x + 20, y = 15x ✓ ·
   y = 20x + 10, y = 15x (the fee and the rate swapped) · y = 10x + 20, y = x + 15 (15 taken as a start) ·
   y = 30x, y = 15x (the fee added to the rate).
7. *Extra number* one story with a number nobody needs and a whole crossing.
8. *Backwards* Two plans cost the same at 6 months: y = 8x + 30 and y = ▢x. The box is 13.
9. *Written* from a graph of two plans: which plan for 10 months, and why — the crossing decides.

**Independent set (kinds; grids printed).** a story's graph read · what the point means · an estimate between the
grid lines · the system for a story · which is cheaper after the crossing · two savers at the same rate: never
equal (no solution).

The Review follows the test's four sections with new numbers and carries an SSDD block (§16 rule 3): four equations
that look alike — parentheses on the left, an x-term on the right — and end four ways (one solution; a negative

three pictures (cross, parallel, same line); a line from a table; reading a crossing and saying what it means.
