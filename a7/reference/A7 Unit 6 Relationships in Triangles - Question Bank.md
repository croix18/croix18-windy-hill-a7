## 6.03  Pythagorean Theorem

*MA.8.GR.1.1*

**Variation.**  Boards 1–2: the hypotenuse, whole then to the tenth; 3–4 a leg, whole then the vote. The pattern breaks at 5 — the triangle turned — and 6 asks only for the equation; 7 is a leg to the tenth; 8 has the extra number; 9 is the check in writing. The independent set: a whole hypotenuse, one to the tenth, a whole leg from a big hypotenuse, a leg to the tenth, a turned triangle, the equation and its value.

**Read this once, when you plan the unit.**

- One book lesson (6.3). Built from the benchmark and the B1G-M first, then read against Math Nation (the unit audit): its partner exploration and matching cards become Notes I–III from the front and board 6.
- Every radicand is a whole number up to 225 with no square factor (GR.1.1, Clarification 3; the unit's standing decision): √n is already in simplest form, and simplifying √50 is Algebra 1. So the only whole hypotenuses are 5, 10, 13 and 15; bigger triples appear as a missing leg (39 and 36 → √225).
- The square corner first, every time: Notes I's triangle and board 5 are turned, so the bottom is not where c is — the state's named misconception for GR.1.1 is misidentifying the hypotenuse.
- Round once, at the end, from the full value: board 2's √97 = 9.849 is 9.8 — rounding twice (9.85, then 9.9) is the error in Math Nation's own 6.04 key (audit D13).

**What the audit against Math Nation found.**

- Every item re-derived by sympy at build time; every irrational answer is checked exactly (√n) and to the tenth separately, and every radicand is held to whole numbers up to 225 with no square factor by the unit's own check beside the kit's.
- Math Nation 6.3 read against this lesson (A7 Unit 6 Audit): its 6.04 ladder (12 ft, 7 ft out) keyed 9.8 for 9.7 is not used here, and board 2's double-rounding trap is that error, named.

**What changed from the book.**

- 6.3.1 (Which One Doesn't Belong) → the warm-up's 10² − 6², the subtraction before it has a name.
- 6.3.5 (partner exploration) → Notes I–III, taught from the front, under the no-partner-work rule.
- 6.3.7 (matching triangles to equations) → board 6, one equation and its named wrong ones.
- 6.03 Additional Practice and Homework → the independent set and IXL under rulings 10 and 21; board 9 is the exit evidence.

The pattern breaks at 4 — slanted, with only the points on the grid — whole, then to the tenth (5), then only the expression (6), then from the origin (7); 8 carries an extra point; 9 is written. The independent set: horizontal, vertical, slanted whole, slanted to the tenth, from the origin, a map.

**Read this once, when you plan the unit.**

- One book lesson (6.5). Built from the benchmark and the B1G-M first, then read against Math Nation (the unit audit): the book teaches no distance formula, and neither do we — the dashed legs are the method.
- The state's named misconception for GR.1.2 is the axis crossing — (−2, 0) to (3, 0) taken as 1, not 5. Notes I teaches it, board 3 votes on it, and board 9 asks for it in writing with new points.

- Every radicand is a whole number up to 225 with no square factor (GR.1.2, Clarification 3), so coordinates stay inside about ±10 and the legs stay small.
