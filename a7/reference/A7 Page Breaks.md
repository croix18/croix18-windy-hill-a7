# Page breaks — Croix's ruling, and what it cost

*Written by the build. Refreshed every time the packages are rebuilt.*

## The ruling

**6 September, Croix: "add the extra page if it's needed."**

Asked whether a question may run over a page break when making it whole would cost a printed
sheet per student per copy, he answered once, for the corpus. A question is not split across a
page. The paper is the price.

## What the build does with it

Every document is rendered and looked at, not argued about. Where a question came out across two
pages, a page break goes in front of it and the document is rendered again — one break at a time,
because each one changes the answer for every break after it. The same pass also moves a question
whose answer box, number line or grid landed on the next page, a stem stranded before its own
first part, and a question whose data table split across the break.

What it will not do is shove a question that cannot fit on one page however it is placed. That
case is rendered, detected, the break is taken back out, and it is listed below instead.

## What it cost

**One page, across both packages.** The Unit 1 unit assessment went from seven pages to eight.
Every other document absorbed its repairs without growing: 924 printed pages before the ruling,
925 after.


## What is left

**Nothing.** Every question in both packages sits whole on one page — 98 documents, no exceptions, and no document the build could not judge.
