# UNIT 4 AUDIT — Math Nation, *Solving Problems with Rational Numbers*

**20 September 2026.** Every problem in the package worked with sympy; about **260 values
re-derived**. The working log beside this file (`UNIT 4 AUDIT - working log.md`) records what was
checked, not only what was wrong. The standards side — benchmark boundaries, every B1G-M task and
item re-derived, the IXL codes — is in `UNIT 4 PRE-AUDIT - standards side.md` and is not repeated
here.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**The unit assessment is clean and the unit's mathematics is mostly sound, but the practice
materials are not safe to hand out as printed.** Fifteen defects, eight of them student-facing.

- **The Unit Assessment and its guide are the best documents in the package** — 13 questions, 24
  values, **zero discrepancies**, a point ledger that totals exactly the 100 it claims, and every
  item inside the benchmark's boundaries. Nothing needs fixing there.
- **Two answer keys in the Teacher Edition are wrong by a factor of ten** (4.4.3 Q1b, 4.4.4 Q1b).
  These are worked answers the teacher reads aloud.
- **Two worked examples do not match the problems they claim to solve** (Homework L3 Q4, where the
  stem's 1.6×10¹¹ and 3.5×10² become 1.6×10¹² and 3.5×10³ in both students' work; Homework L1 Q4,
  where the scaffold computes with 6.8 while the stem says 4.2).
- **One practice item has no correct answer at all** (Practice L1 Q1).
- **Two false facts about the physical world**: the speed of light given as 3×10⁹ m/s, and a 2020
  smartphone clocked at 2.7 MHz. The first is worse than it looks — the answer the book *circles
  as the error* is the physically correct one.
- **One rule is stated wrongly**: the significant-digit rule for products is stated as if it
  governed sums and differences too. Every example the book uses happens to come out the same
  either way, so nothing in the book is numerically wrong — but the rule is, and a student who
  learns it will be wrong elsewhere.
- **Four items sit outside the benchmark's own boundary** — addition and subtraction with the two
  exponents more than 2 apart — and one of them is two questions away from a margin note in which
  the book quotes that very boundary.

Nothing here is unusable. Every context worth keeping can be kept once the numbers are repaired,
and the assessment is a genuinely good model for ours.

**One gap in this audit, stated plainly.** The package as uploaded contains no Practice or
Homework answer keys, no standalone Student Edition, no Additional Practice and no Stepping
Stones. **The Practice and Homework items were worked and judged; their keys could not be
checked, because they were not supplied.** In Unit 3 the worst defects were key errors, so this
is a real hole. If those keys exist, send them and I will audit them in an hour.

## 3. Defects — teacher-facing only

- **TE 4.5.4 and 4.7.3 margins** tell teachers students "will not have a calculator on the **FSA**
  for this benchmark." The test is the **FAST**, and Florida provides an on-screen scientific
  calculator at grades 7–8 (Source of Truth, Part 3). Two errors in one sentence.
- **TE 4.6.3 Q2** advice: "any number raised to the third power means to multiply the number
  itself three times" — loose; a number used as a factor three times is multiplied twice.
- **4.4.1 Warm-Up** keys a total of 6.4 × 10¹⁹ for a sum whose exact value is
  6.400000012 × 10¹⁹, with no "answers may vary" line on an open-ended task that has many
  solutions.

## 4. Conflicts with our settled rules (not errors — just what changes in the rebuild)

- **Partner and group work is pervasive**: 4.1.2 and 4.3.2 Explorations (4.3.2 also has students
  collect initials from two other pairs), **4.2.4 "Give Me Five!"** — a two-player game with
  problem cards and a gameboard, 4.4.2 partner discussions, 4.6.3 and 4.7.2 Collaborations, and
  the whole of **Lesson 8, which is three rotating stations**. All taught from the front or
  rewritten as written work, exactly as Units 2 and 3 were.
- **Calculators.** "Students should not be allowed to use a calculator" (4.2.1), "The questions on
  the unit assessment are no calculator" (Assessment Guide p. 2), "students should **not** use a
  calculator" (4.6.5), "consider a four function calculator… not a scientific calculator" (4.7.3,
  4.8.2). Croix's ruling is the opposite and nothing about calculators is printed on any of our
  pages. The obligation this creates falls on the item, not the page.
- **Video/affective warm-ups**: 4.7.1 is a Mark Cuban quotation and an acronym exercise with no
  mathematics in it. Under ruling 31 it is replaced by spaced retrieval, like every warm-up.
- **"Homework"** as a word, and the TE's repeated "if time is an issue, assign Your Turn as
  homework."
- **Reflection wrap-ups** (4.1.6, 4.4.5, 4.5.6, 4.7.5, 4.8.5 — "muddiest point", interest grids,
  thumbs-up self-assessment) are five of the eight lessons' closers. Under ruling 10 the period
  ends with the whiteboard round and IXL; under ruling 21 the independent set now sits between
  them.

## 5. Capcheck — items outside the benchmark boundary

MA.8.NSO.1.5 and 1.6 limit **addition and subtraction** to exponents **within 2 of each other**.

| Where | Item | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Homework L1 Q2** | (8.7 × 10⁶) + (4.8 × 10³) | **3** |
| **Homework L4 Q3a** | 1.898 × 10¹¹ + 2.37 × 10¹⁴ | **3** |
| **Homework L4 Q3c** | 2.37 × 10¹⁴ − 1.898 × 10¹¹ | **3** |
| **TE 4.1.4 Q3** | 2.37 × 10³² + 14.29 × 10³⁴ — written properly, 2.37×10³² + 1.429×10³⁵ | **3** |

The last one is the striking one: the margin note on the facing page quotes Clarification 1
("exponents are limited to within two of each other") and says the lesson has built understanding
to within one. The book states the rule and breaks it two questions later.

Everything else is inside the boundary. Every addition and subtraction on the **assessment** is
within 2 (gaps of 1, 2, 2, 2, 1). Every radicand in Lessons 5–8 is a perfect square up to 225 or
a perfect cube within −125 to 125, or the rational forms 1/8, 1/27, 1/125, 1/9 — defensible, since
numerator and denominator are each perfect and within range, but a case our own capcheck has to be
told about rather than left to guess.

**The book's own assessment limits for MA.8.NSO.1.7** (Assessment Guide p. 1) are tighter than the
B1G-M and worth adopting: expressions must be *given*; they must contain a negative exponent
and/or a radical; the radicand is a perfect square or perfect cube; **integer exponents between
−3 and 3 inclusive**. Every 8.NSO.1.7 item on the assessment obeys them.

## 6. What the state guide expects that Math Nation never does

These go into our banks. All values re-derived (pre-audit §6).

1. **Comparisons that cross from very large to very small.** 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 3 × 10⁻⁴ = 10¹². Every
   comparison in the book stays on one side.
2. **The renormalization misconception as a named distractor.** The guide names "leaving 12×10⁹
   instead of 1.2×10¹⁰"; the book teaches the repair (Celia, Dominic, Mara) but never offers it as
   a wrong option to be recognized.
3. **The two multiplication/addition confusions as distractors**: (1.3×10³)+(3.4×10⁵) = 4.7×10⁸
   and (2×10⁴)(3×10⁵) = 6×10²⁰.
4. **The meteorite task** (1.1×10², 6.8×10², 8.4×10⁻² g): difference 679.916 g; 8,095 times;
   total 790.084 g fits in 850 g. A four-part item on one set of numbers — an SSDD block ready-made.
5. **The correct rule for sums and differences**, stated beside the product rule so students can
   tell them apart. The book never distinguishes them (D13).
6. **The Dotson backyard** (√200 fence, panel unit prices) — a genuine multi-step order-of-
   operations task with a decision at the end.
7. **PEMDAS is to be avoided** — the guide says so explicitly, and 4.5.2's refresher numbers the
   four steps without the mnemonic. Our notes teach the structure: grouping symbols (the radical
   bar and the fraction bar included), then powers and roots together, then × ÷ left to right,
   then + − left to right. The word PEMDAS appears nowhere in the unit.

## 7. Structure check against the scope and sequence

Eight book lessons over **six teaching days**, with 4.2 + 4.3 merged and 4.7 + 4.8 merged — both
merges already in the scope and sequence under ruling 30. 4.05 and 4.06 stay separate days.
No thread day falls inside Unit 4. The unit is the second of three under MA.8.NSO and all three
benchmarks report under Number Sense and Operations, so the review's parts split by benchmark.
IXL lists the **same skill (D8J) for both 4.05 and 4.06**; under ruling 28 that is one assignment
covering the run, due after the second of the two lessons.

Under ruling 27 the assessment is **one paper over two days**, so the calendar needs day 1 on a
Monday or Thursday with day 2 the next school day, the review the school day before, and no
Wednesday. The current scope has the exam on **Wed 21 – Thu 22 October**, which ruling 27
disallows. That is a `tools/scope_calendar.py` fix, listed with the ruling work.

## 8. Judgment calls

Only one, and I have a default for it.

**The significant-digit rule for sums and differences.** The book states the product/quotient rule
and applies it to subtraction (D13); its own examples come out the same either way, and the
assessment's Q2 does too. **Default: we teach it correctly** — least *decimal places* for sums and
differences, least *significant digits* for products and quotients, side by side, with one example
where they differ. The benchmark's clarification only says to recognize the importance of
significant digits when physical measurements are involved, so teaching it correctly stays aligned,
and Rule 0 settles the rest. Say the word if you would rather we match the book.

were raised with the rulings and are not Unit 4 questions.)*
