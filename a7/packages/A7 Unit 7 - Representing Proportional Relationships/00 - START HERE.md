## Standing decisions baked into these files

- **No partner or group work anywhere.** Math Nation's explorations and collaborations are taught from the front; its partner-discussion tasks are written whiteboard questions.
- **Calculators: allowed on everything, and nothing about them is printed on any student page.** The one exception the house style allows is the Reference Sheet's description of what the FAST platform provides (an on-screen scientific calculator) — that is a description, not a permission line.
- **Old-school textbook styling.** No commentary panels, no grey bars, no clip art. American spellings.
- **One point per lettered part, no partial credit**, with follow-through credit (ruling 19): a later part earns its point when the right operation is applied to the student's own earlier value, where that work is on the page. The key carries a `Scoring:` line under every question and a per-benchmark Score Tracker at the end.
- **The assessment is one paper, one class period** (ruling 51, 24 October 2026 — every A7 unit test is one period; the practice test's random draw is sized for it, ruling 49). No transfer items: a random set can only ask what the practice asks (ruling 49).
- **No benchmark codes on student pages.** The deck title slide and the keys and Lesson Plans carry them.
- **Opens cleanly in Google Docs.** Every document is a fixed-width table layout with no nested tables and a spacer after every block; every document and every slide is set in Lexend (a Google font), with FreeSerif for the bubbles only.
- **Every answer is re-derived by machine at build time.** Every item carries a `check` that sympy evaluates (variable bases as symbols); every multiple-choice distractor must name its error; every scientific-notation coefficient is scanned for the [1, 10) rule; the build refuses on any finding. The post-build suite (`checks.py`) then reads the rendered PDFs for off-page text, glyph coverage, Google-Docs layout rules and the timing plan.
- **Nothing here is taken from Math Nation.** The book was read for the unit's scope and the shapes of its test's questions only; every lesson and question is built from the benchmark text and the B1G-M. The plan and the review's rulings are in `Reference/`.

## Before the unit starts

- **Built in the order the rulebook asks (§16):** the test was settled first, from the benchmarks (MA.7.AR.4.1–4.5 and MA.8.AR.3.1), then the lessons were built to it. Math Nation's Unit 7 was read for scope and its test's item shapes only; nothing is taken from it. The plan was read by a Barton reader and a FAST reader before any spec was written.
- **One definition, said the same way every day:** two quantities are in a proportional relationship when one quantity is always a constant multiple of the other, y = px. p is the constant of proportionality (named in 7.02–03; on 7.01 it is the unit rate). Florida writes p; "some books write k" is said once, in 7.05–06.
- **Three tests, in these words:** table — y ÷ x is the same for every pair; graph — a straight line through the origin; description — a constant rate with nothing added at the start. From 7.04, the equation: y = px with nothing added. No cross products (the state: they "may lead to errors").
- **"Slope" and "y-intercept" are on no page.** Unit 11 graphed lines from a table and named the coefficient of x; Unit 10 names slope on 19 Jan. A steeper line is "more y for every 1 unit of x"; a line that misses the origin has "a number added".
- **Which quantity is y is said in every item,** and a constant carries its unit as y-unit per x-unit. Either quantity may be y (m = 60h, h = (1/60)m): taught in 7.05–06 and asked once on the test (question 12b), with the direction named.
- **Graphs have readable points and an axis scale is not always 1:** "read the scales first" from 7.01, a graph scaled by 2, 4, 5, 10 or 20 in every lesson, and the test's question 2 turns on a d-axis scaled by 0.5. Where a choice question shows several graphs, they share one set of axes and scales. A context is drawn in Quadrant I; one negative constant is taught (7.02–03) and tested (question 6).
- **Whiteboards read; the Independent Set draws.** 7.01's handout prints empty grids, and its question 1 is drawn in class with the teacher circulating. The test has one draw-the-graph question (1b), whose key prints the graph.
- **MA.8.AR.3.1 lives in 7.04 and in one test question (9):** a linear relationship is also proportional only when its line passes through the origin — Unit 11's y = 2x + 3 versus y = 2x. Calculators are allowed and never mentioned.
- **Hand-off to Unit 10 (from 15 Jan) — write it into that unit's plan:** it names the constant of proportionality as the slope of y = px, and reopens 7.04's pair (y = 2x and y = 2x + 3) for MA.8.AR.3.1 with that word.
- **Hand-off to circles (MA.7.GR.1.3), when taught:** π is the constant of proportionality between a circle's circumference and its diameter.
- **Hand-off to the practice page (not built in this pass):** question 1(b) needs a part where the student plots points.
