## PART 0 — THE COURSE — CONFIRMED

This was the first thing to verify and it changes everything downstream.

### Course identity — RESOLVED

| | |
|---|---|
| Course number | **1205050** |
| Official title | **Grade 7 Accelerated Mathematics** (course-code abbreviation *M/J GR 7 ACCEL MATH*; CPALMS also displays it as *M/J Accelerated Mathematics Grade 7*) |
| Version description | *"In Grade 7 Accelerated Mathematics, instructional time will emphasize six areas: (1) representing numbers in scientific notation and extending the set of numbers to the system of real numbers, which includes irrational numbers; (2) generating equivalent numeric and algebraic expressions including using the Laws of Exponents; (3) creating and reasoning about linear relationships including modeling an association in bivariate data with a linear equation; (4) solving linear equations, inequalities and systems of linear equations; (5) developing an understanding of the concept of a function and (6) analyzing two-dimensional figures, particularly triangles, using distance, angle and applying the Pythagorean Theorem."* |

Read that description carefully: **all six emphases are grade 8 content.** Not one of them is a grade 7 topic. The grade 7 benchmarks the course carries are there to fill gaps, not to define the course.

### Which test — RESOLVED

**Grade 8 FAST Mathematics.** Not the Grade 7 FAST, and no EOC.

Three independent lines of evidence:

1. **The state's participation rule is by course content, not enrolled grade.** FLDOE Statewide Assessment Update (May 24, 2022), slide 13: *"As with FSA, students should participate in the FAST assessment that best aligns with the content of the course in which they are receiving instruction (e.g., if an enrolled 6th grader is taking a 7th grade advanced math course, they should take the Grade 7 FAST Mathematics Assessment)."*
2. **Every benchmark on the Grade 8 FAST blueprint is in course 1205050.** The course carries the complete MA.8 set — all 40 benchmarks. There is no grade 8 benchmark the course omits.
3. **District course catalogs say so outright.** Pasco County's catalog entry for 1205050 names the terminal assessment as the *"8th Grade Pre-Algebra F.A.S.T Assessment."*

> **Honest limit:** no FLDOE document names course 1205050 and its required assessment in a single sentence. The course description PDF contains no assessment statement at all. The three lines above make Grade 8 FAST the only defensible reading, but if the department ever pushes back, the Pasco catalog line and the FOIL slide are what to hand them.

**After this course** these students normally take Algebra 1 in grade 8 and the B.E.S.T. Algebra 1 EOC.

### Exactly which benchmarks the course carries

**57 content benchmarks: all 40 grade 8, plus 17 selected grade 7.** Plus the seven MA.K12.MTR.x.1 thinking-and-reasoning standards, which are not separately assessed.

**All 40 grade 8 benchmarks** (MA.8.NSO.1.1–1.7 · MA.8.AR.1.1–1.3 · MA.8.AR.2.1–2.3 · MA.8.AR.3.1–3.5 · MA.8.AR.4.1–4.3 · MA.8.F.1.1–1.3 · MA.8.GR.1.1–1.6 · MA.8.GR.2.1–2.4 · MA.8.DP.1.1–1.3 · MA.8.DP.2.1–2.3).

### What the course DROPS — read this before revising any lesson

These grade 7 benchmarks are **not** in course 1205050 and **not** on the Grade 8 FAST:

- **MA.7.NSO.2.1, 2.2, 2.3** — order of operations with rationals; four-operation fluency; real-world operations
- **MA.7.AR.1.1, 1.2** — adding/subtracting linear expressions; equivalence of linear expressions
- **MA.7.AR.2.1** — one-step inequalities
- **MA.7.AR.3.1** — multi-step percent problems (discounts, markups, interest, percent error, percent change)
- **MA.7.AR.3.2** — proportions in real-world problems
- **MA.7.GR.1.1, 1.2** — areas of trapezoids/parallelograms/rhombi; area of composite polygons
- **MA.7.DP.1.1, 1.2, 1.3** — measures of center and variation; comparing two data sets; sample-to-population prediction
- **MA.7.DP.2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4** — the entire grade 7 probability strand

**Two consequences that will come up repeatedly:**

**1. The probability foundation is skipped.** Grade 8 probability (MA.8.DP.2.1–2.3) is *repeated* experiments — it assumes simple sample space, theoretical probability, and experimental-vs-theoretical are already known. Course 1205050 never teaches them. If a Math Nation lesson quietly assumes them, that is a prerequisite gap to name in the teacher's edition, not a defect in the lesson. **The entire on-level Unit 2 build does not transfer to this class** — different benchmarks, different depth.

**2. Some Math Nation 7 Accelerated lessons will be off-blueprint.** Math Nation writes to its own scope and sequence. When a lesson lands on a dropped benchmark — percents, measures of center, area of a trapezoid — it is enrichment, not tested content. **Flag it in the defect list and say so; do not silently cut it.** That is a curriculum decision, not a wording one.

**3. "Dropped" means dropped from THIS course, not from the student's schooling.** Course **1205020, M/J Grade 6 Accelerated Mathematics**, carries the complementary grade 7 benchmarks — verified against the FLDOE course description at `https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/7576/urlt/Grade-6-Accelerated.pdf`: **17 MA.7 benchmarks, including the whole of MA.7.DP.2 (probability) and MA.7.AR.3.1 and 3.2 (percents and proportions), and excluding MA.7.NSO.1.2 and MA.7.AR.2.2**, which are 1205050's. So a student who came through the accelerated track in grade 6 has met the probability foundation and the percent work; a student who transferred in from on-level grade 6 has not. The two lists are complementary by design and neither is complete on its own. **This changes what a prerequisite gap means:** consequence 1 above is a gap for the class as a whole only if the class did not come through 1205020, and that is a question about a roster, not about a benchmark list. Ask before writing "students have never seen this" into a teacher's edition.

#### NUMBER SENSE AND OPERATIONS

**MA.8.NSO.1.1** — Extend previous understanding of rational numbers to define irrational numbers within the real number system. Locate an approximate value of a numerical expression involving irrational numbers on a number line.
- *Clarification 1:* instruction includes the use of number line and rational number approximations, and recognizing pi (π) as an irrational number.
- **Boundary:** approximate numerical expressions involving **one arithmetic operation**, estimating square roots or π.

**MA.8.NSO.1.2** — Plot, order and compare rational and irrational numbers, represented in various forms.
- **Boundary:** **not** the expectation to work with the number *e*.
- **Boundary:** the expectation is to plot, order and compare **square roots and cube roots**.
- *Clarification:* the expectation is to use symbols (<, > or =).

**MA.8.NSO.1.3** — Extend previous understanding of the Laws of Exponents to include integer exponents. Apply the Laws of Exponents to evaluate numerical expressions and generate equivalent numerical expressions, limited to **integer exponents and rational number bases**, with procedural fluency.
- *Clarification:* refer to the K-12 Formulas (Appendix E) for the Laws of Exponents.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** includes negative integer exponents; **does not include fractional exponents.**
- *Vertical:* Gr 7 was whole-number exponents with whole-number results → Gr 8 adds integer exponents.

**MA.8.NSO.1.4** — Express numbers in scientific notation to represent and approximate very large or very small quantities. Determine how many times larger or smaller one number is compared to a second number.

**MA.8.NSO.1.5** — Add, subtract, multiply and divide numbers expressed in scientific notation with procedural fluency.
- **Boundary:** for **addition and subtraction**, exponents are limited to **within 2 of each other**.

**MA.8.NSO.1.6** — Solve real-world problems involving operations with numbers expressed in scientific notation.
- *Clarification:* includes recognizing the importance of **significant digits** when physical measurements are involved.
- **Boundary:** same within-2 exponent limit on addition and subtraction.

**MA.8.NSO.1.7** — Solve multi-step mathematical and real-world problems involving the order of operations with rational numbers including exponents and radicals.
- **Boundary:** **6 or fewer steps.**
- **Boundary:** simplify radicals by factoring **square roots of perfect squares up to 225** and **cube roots of perfect cubes from −125 to 125**.

---

#### ALGEBRAIC REASONING

**MA.8.AR.1.1** — Apply the Laws of Exponents to generate equivalent algebraic expressions, limited to **integer exponents and monomial bases**.
- *Clarification:* refer to the K-12 Formulas (Appendix E).
- *B1G-M definition to use:* "a base may be a product of a coefficient and one or more variables with integer exponents."

**MA.8.AR.1.2** — Apply properties of operations to multiply two linear expressions with rational coefficients.
- **Boundary:** products where **at least one factor is a monomial**.

**MA.8.AR.1.3** — Rewrite the sum of two algebraic expressions having a common monomial factor as a common factor multiplied by the sum of two algebraic expressions.

**MA.8.AR.2.1** — Solve multi-step linear equations in one variable, with rational number coefficients. **Include equations with variables on both sides.**
- *Clarification:* problem types include equations that generate **one solution, infinitely many solutions or no solution**.

**MA.8.AR.2.2** — Solve two-step linear inequalities in one variable and represent solutions algebraically and graphically.
- **Boundary — forms only:** *px ± q > r* and *p(x ± q) > r*, p/q/r specific rational numbers, **any inequality symbol**.
- Variable may be on **either side**.

**MA.8.AR.2.3** — Given an equation in the form of *x² = p* and *x³ = q*, where p is a whole number and q is an integer, determine the real solutions.
- *Clarification:* focus on understanding that solving *x² = p* gives **both a positive and a negative solution**.
- **Boundary:** square roots of perfect squares **up to 225**; cube roots of perfect cubes **from −125 to 125**.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** students are **not** required to isolate the x² or x³ term.

**MA.8.AR.3.1** — Determine if a linear relationship is also a proportional relationship.
- *Clarification:* proportional relationships are linear relationships **whose graph passes through the origin**.
- *Clarification:* includes tables, graphs, equations and written descriptions.
- **Assessment note:** this benchmark has **no blueprint row of its own** — see Part 2.

**MA.8.AR.3.2** — Given a table, graph or written description of a linear relationship, determine the slope.
- *Clarification:* includes cases where **two points** are given.
- *Clarification:* connects slope to the constant of proportionality and to **similar triangles on the coordinate plane**.
- *B1G-M:* "slope, which represents a constant rate of change, … is not the same as a constant of proportionality unless the relationship goes through the origin."

**MA.8.AR.3.3** — Given a table, graph or written description of a linear relationship, write an equation in slope-intercept form.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** *"Point-slope form and standard forms are not expectations at this grade level."*
- *B1G-M:* give equations in formats other than *y = mx + b*, such as *y = ax + b* or *y = b + mx*.

**MA.8.AR.3.4** — Given a mathematical or real-world context, graph a two-variable linear equation from a written description, a table or an equation in slope-intercept form.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** point-slope and standard form are not expectations.

**MA.8.AR.3.5** — Given a real-world context, determine and interpret the slope and y-intercept of a two-variable linear equation from a written description, a table, a graph or an equation in slope-intercept form.
- *Clarification:* problems include **temperature conversions** and **equations of lines of fit in scatter plots**.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** *"it is not the expectation to compare slopes or y-intercepts of two linear equations."* Memorizing the F/C conversion formulas is **not** expected.

**MA.8.AR.4.1** — Given a system of two linear equations and a specified set of possible solutions, determine which ordered pairs satisfy the system of linear equations.
- *Clarification:* a solution satisfies **both** equations simultaneously.

**MA.8.AR.4.2** — Given a system of two linear equations represented graphically on the same coordinate plane, determine whether there is one solution, no solution or infinitely many solutions.

**MA.8.AR.4.3** — Given a mathematical or real-world context, solve systems of two linear equations **by graphing**.
- *Clarification:* includes **approximating non-integer solutions**.
- **Boundary:** systems represented in **slope-intercept form only**.
- *Clarification:* includes recognizing that parallel lines have the same slope.
- **Note:** substitution and elimination are Algebra 1. Graphing only at grade 8.

#### FUNCTIONS — new at grade 8, no grade 7 equivalent

**MA.8.F.1.1** — Given a set of ordered pairs, a table, a graph or mapping diagram, determine whether the relationship is a function. **Identify the domain and range of the relation.**
- *Clarification:* refer to the input as the **independent variable** and the output as the **dependent variable**.
- **Boundary:** represent domain and range **as a list of numbers or as an inequality**. A list cannot represent a relation with infinitely many values; an inequality cannot represent a discrete set.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** function **notation f(x) is Algebra 1**, not grade 8. *"In Algebra 1, students will classify the function type and represent it using function notation."*
- ⚠ **Florida cautions against the vertical line test** — see Part 5.

**MA.8.F.1.2** — Given a function defined by a graph or an equation, determine whether the function is a **linear** function. Given an input-output table, determine whether it **could** represent a linear function.
- *Clarification:* includes recognizing that **a table may not determine a function**.

**MA.8.F.1.3** — Analyze a real-world written description or graphical representation of a functional relationship between two quantities and identify where the function is **increasing, decreasing or constant**.
- **Boundary:** **continuous functions only.**
- *Clarification:* includes writing a description of a graph, or sketching a graph from a written description.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** students are **not** expected to recognize curves representing exponential growth or decay.

#### GEOMETRIC REASONING

**MA.8.GR.1.1** — Apply the Pythagorean Theorem to solve mathematical and real-world problems involving unknown side lengths in right triangles.
- *Clarification:* explore right triangles with natural-number side lengths to illustrate the theorem.
- **⚠ The expectation is to MEMORIZE the Pythagorean Theorem.** It is not on the reference sheet.
- **Boundary:** radicands limited to **whole numbers up to 225**.

**MA.8.GR.1.2** — Apply the Pythagorean Theorem to solve mathematical and real-world problems involving the **distance between two points in a coordinate plane**.
- **⚠ Memorize the theorem. It is NOT the expectation to use the distance formula.**
- *Clarification:* connect coordinate distance to right triangles.
- **Boundary:** radicands ≤ 225.

**MA.8.GR.1.3** — Use the **Triangle Inequality Theorem** to determine if a triangle can be formed from a given set of sides. Use the **converse of the Pythagorean Theorem** to determine if a right triangle can be formed from a given set of sides.

**MA.8.GR.1.4** — Solve mathematical problems involving the relationships between **supplementary, complementary, vertical or adjacent** angles.

**MA.8.GR.1.5** — Solve problems involving the relationships of **interior and exterior angles of a triangle**.
- *Clarification:* includes the **Triangle Sum Theorem** and representing angle measures as **algebraic expressions**.

**MA.8.GR.1.6** — Develop and use formulas for the **sums of the interior angles of regular polygons** by decomposing them into triangles.
- *Clarification:* includes representing angle measures as algebraic expressions.
- Students **develop** (n − 2)180°; it is not given.

**MA.8.GR.2.1** — Given a preimage and image generated by a **single transformation**, identify the transformation that describes the relationship.
- **Boundary:** reflections, translations or rotations only (no dilations here).
- *Clarification:* focus on **preservation of congruence**.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** *not* on the coordinate plane — that is GR.2.3. A single transformation means **one** vertical *or* **one** horizontal translation.

**MA.8.GR.2.2** — Given a preimage and image generated by a **single dilation**, identify the **scale factor** that describes the relationship.
- *Clarification:* connects to scale drawings and proportions.
- *Clarification:* similarity is preserved; congruence is **not**, unless the scale factor is 1.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** not on the coordinate plane. *"The preimage and its vertices will not have prime notation."*

**MA.8.GR.2.3** — Describe and apply the effect of a **single transformation** on two-dimensional figures using coordinates and the coordinate plane.
- **Boundary:** reflections, translations, rotations or dilations.
- **Boundary:** lines of reflection limited to the **x-axis, y-axis, or lines parallel to the axes**.
- **Boundary:** rotations **about the origin only**, limited to **90°, 180°, 270°, 360°**.
- **Boundary:** dilations **centered at the origin only**.
- **Boundary (B1G-M):** a vertical *and* horizontal translation counts as **two** transformations — out of scope.

**MA.8.GR.2.4** — Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving proportional relationships between **similar triangles**.

#### DATA ANALYSIS AND PROBABILITY

**MA.8.DP.1.1** — Given a set of real-world **bivariate numerical** data, construct a **scatter plot or a line graph** as appropriate for the context.
- *Clarification:* includes recognizing similarities and differences between the two and choosing based on context.
- **Boundary:** data sets limited to **20 points**.

**MA.8.DP.1.2** — Given a scatter plot within a real-world context, describe **patterns of association**.
- *Clarification:* descriptions include **outliers; positive or negative; linear or nonlinear; strong or weak**.
- *B1G-M:* an association usually needs **more than one descriptor** — "strong, positive, linear," not just "positive."
- *B1G-M:* *"Outliers in scatter plots are different than outliers in box plots. There is no special rule determining if a data point is an outlier in a scatter plot."*

**MA.8.DP.1.3** — Given a scatter plot with a linear association, **informally fit a straight line**.
- *Clarification:* focus on the connection to linear functions.
- *Clarification:* use tools including a ruler; draw a line with **approximately the same number of points above and below**.
- No regression, no formula. "Informally" is the operative word.

**MA.8.DP.2.1** — Determine the sample space for a **repeated** experiment.
- *Clarification:* record sample spaces using **organized lists, tables or tree diagrams**.
- **Boundary — the experiments allowed:** tossing a fair coin · rolling a fair die · picking a card randomly from a deck **with replacement** · picking marbles randomly from a bag **with replacement** · spinning a fair spinner. **That list is closed.**
- **Boundary:** **repetition limited to two times, except for tossing a coin.**

**MA.8.DP.2.2** — Find the **theoretical probability** of an event related to a repeated experiment.
- *Clarification:* represent probability as a fraction, percentage or decimal. Uses **P(event)** notation.
- Same two boundaries as DP.2.1.
- **Assessment note:** no blueprint row of its own — see Part 2.

**MA.8.DP.2.3** — Solve real-world problems involving probabilities related to **single or repeated** experiments, including **making predictions based on theoretical probability**.
- *Clarification:* connects to proportional relationships; P(event) notation.
- Same two boundaries as DP.2.1.
- Note this is the only grade 8 probability benchmark that mentions **single** experiments — and it is the one with a blueprint row.

## PART 2 — THE GRADE 8 FAST

### Structure

- **Adaptive**, computer-based.
- **Three administrations:** PM1 (start of year, baseline), PM2 (mid-year), PM3 (spring, summative). *"While PM1 and PM2 are for informational purposes only, PM3 is used for school accountability."*
- **36–40 items**, covering **38 benchmark groupings**.
- **Field-test items:** *"approximately 4-5 items … are experimental (field test) items and are included in the ranges above but are not included in students' scores."* They are counted **inside** the 36–40, not added to it.
- **Time, grades 6–8 mathematics:** up to **100 minutes** (PM1, PM2); up to **120 minutes** (PM3). Single block — there is no session split.

### Grade 8 reporting categories and weights

| Reporting category | % of points | Blueprint rows |
|---|---|---|
| Number Sense and Operations **and Probability** | **22–28%** | 9 |
| Algebraic Reasoning | **22–28%** | 9 |
| Linear Relationships, Data Analysis, **and Functions** | **25–31%** | 10 |
| Geometric Reasoning | **22–28%** | 10 |
| | | **38** |

**Note the two category names that surprise people.** Probability is bundled into Number Sense, not into Data Analysis. Functions is bundled with Linear Relationships and Data Analysis, and that combined category is the **heaviest** on the test. Contrast grade 7, where Proportional Reasoning was the heavy category.

Row membership in document order:
- **NSO and Probability:** MA.8.NSO.1.1–1.7 · MA.8.DP.2.1 · MA.8.DP.2.3
- **Algebraic Reasoning:** MA.8.AR.1.1–1.3 · MA.8.AR.2.1–2.3 · MA.8.AR.4.1–4.3
- **Linear Relationships, Data Analysis and Functions:** MA.8.F.1.1 · MA.8.F.1.2 · MA.8.F.1.3 · MA.8.AR.3.2–3.5 · MA.8.DP.1.1–1.3
- **Geometric Reasoning:** MA.8.GR.1.1–1.6 · MA.8.GR.2.1–2.4

### "Also Assesses" pairings — CONFIRMED

Exactly two grade 8 benchmarks have no row of their own:

| Benchmark | Assessed under | Category |
|---|---|---|
| **MA.8.DP.2.2** (theoretical probability of a repeated experiment) | **MA.8.DP.2.3** | NSO and Probability |
| **MA.8.AR.3.1** (is a linear relationship proportional?) | **MA.8.F.1.2** | Linear Relationships, Data Analysis and Functions |

The second pairing is worth sitting with. **MA.8.AR.3.1 is assessed through a Functions item**, not an Algebraic Reasoning one — it is the only AR.3.x benchmark without its own row. Practical read: *"is this proportional?"* will arrive dressed as *"is this a linear function?"* Build practice that way.

Likewise **MA.8.DP.2.2 is never tested in isolation** — theoretical probability of a repeated experiment always arrives inside a real-world prediction problem.

### Calculator — RESOLVED

**A calculator is available at every Grade 8 benchmark. There is no calculator-free portion of the Grade 8 FAST.**

## PART 4 — FAST HOUSE STYLE (grade 8 items)

Source: FLDOE's **2025 annual test release** for Grade 8 FAST Mathematics — 36 real operational items with benchmark, key, and statewide percent-correct. This is a better source than the sample-item packet because it shows what actually appeared, and how hard it was.

*(The `flfast.org` sample-item packets are robots-blocked and could not be retrieved. See Part 7.)*

### The patterns worth copying

1. **"An expression is shown." is the house style, and grade 8 uses it constantly.** Standalone declarative sentence → stimulus on its own line → question. Observed forms: *"An expression is shown." · "An equation is shown." · "An inequality is shown." · "A graph of a line is shown." · "Three values are shown." · "Some points on a line are shown in the table." · "A figure is shown with three lines passing through point G."* Passive variants: *"The two figures shown are congruent." · "The right triangle shown has side lengths of x, y, and z units."*
2. **Stems are extremely short.** Most are one sentence, often under twelve words: *"Which value is equivalent to 5⁻³?"* Context items get at most two sentences. **There is not one multi-paragraph stem in the entire grade 8 release.**
3. **Units are declared parenthetically in the setup, then repeated inside the question.** *"…with side lengths in feet (ft)"* → *"What is the length, in feet, of QU?"*
4. **Multiselect never states how many to pick.** All three multiselect items read simply *"Select all the…"* and their keys have 3, 2 and 2 answers. This is a deliberate difficulty lever — one of them scored 26%.
5. **In multiselect the direction leads, it does not trail.** *"Select all the solutions to x³ = 64."*
6. **No Part A / Part B in the grade 8 release.** Multi-response demand is carried instead by matching grids, two-dropdown sentence completion, and two-blank equation editors.
7. **Every graph is a stimulus to read, never a canvas to draw on.** Students choose among four pre-drawn graphs rather than plotting. The GRID and graphing tools exist in the platform but were not exercised in the released set.
8. **Equation editor has one twist:** when the answer is a value of a named variable, the response area is prefixed **"x ="**. When it is a measurement or count, it is a bare box. Scientific notation uses **inline blanks inside the equation** — *"= ▢ × 10^▢"* — introduced by *"Complete the equation to show the quotient in scientific notation."*
9. **Matching items are grids with a letter in every cell**, running A, B, C / D, E, F / G, H, I. Rows are objects, columns are candidate values. Keys are reported as letter triples.
10. **Editing Task Choice uses dropdowns embedded mid-sentence**, introduced by *"Complete the sentence about…"*
11. **Names are single first names, no surnames, no descriptors, and they are rare** — only three appear in 36 items: Mackenzie, Charlie, Sal.
12. **Variables rotate:** x and y dominate; z for a third side; h for height; p and q for monomials. Prime notation (′) is used freely for transformation images with no explanation.
13. **Distractors are systematic error products, not noise.** One exponent item's four options are exactly the four ways to mishandle a power of a product.
14. **Nothing warms up.** No greeting, no transition, no "Now let's…", no "Remember that…". The item starts.

### How hard grade 8 items actually are

Of the 36 released items, **only two cleared 65% correct**. Six sat at or below 26%. The lowest scores clustered on:

| Skill | % correct |
|---|---|
| Distributive property with decimal coefficients (MA.8.AR.1.2) | **21%** |
| Dividing in scientific notation (MA.8.NSO.1.5) | **22%** |
| Pythagorean Theorem in a real-world figure (MA.8.GR.1.1) | **25%** |
| Multiplying monomials (MA.8.AR.1.1) | 25% |
| Selecting all solutions to x³ = 64 (MA.8.AR.2.3) | 26% |
| Slope from a table (MA.8.AR.3.2) | 26% |

**This is the most actionable page in the whole file.** These six are where to spend extra worksheet real estate. Note that four of the six are computational fluency, not concept — which is an argument against thinning the practice sets in those benchmarks even for an accelerated group.

### The contrast with Math Nation

Math Nation opens with a scenario paragraph, embeds numbers in prose, then asks a compound question. FAST does the opposite at every step. Revising toward the patterns above is a **wording** change, not a difficulty change.

## PART 5 — FLORIDA'S OWN WORDING RULES (grade 8)

Source: the grade 8 **B.E.S.T. Instructional Guide for Mathematics (B1G-M)**. The FLDOE PDF truncates around page 50 (mid MA.8.AR.2.3) on every fetch path; the remainder was recovered from CPALMS `PreviewStandard/Export/<ID>`, which republishes the identical benchmark instructional guide text. **All 40 benchmarks were covered.**

### Explicit prohibitions

1. **⚠ Be careful with the vertical line test.** This is the grade 8 headline, and it is the analogue of grade 7's "no cross-multiplying." MA.8.F.1.1: *"The 'vertical line test' should be treated with caution because (1) it allows you to apply a rule without thinking and (2) it may create misconceptions for later mathematics."*
2. **No PEMDAS.** MA.8.NSO.1.7: *"Avoid mnemonics, such as PEMDAS, that do not account for other grouping symbols and do not exercise proper number sense that allows for calculating accurately in a different order."*
3. **No keyword strategies / CUBES.** Named as a misconception under MA.8.NSO.1.7: students *"incorrectly oversimplify a problem by circling the numbers, underlining the question, boxing in key words, and eliminating important contextual information."*
4. **Variables are not limited to x.** MA.8.AR.2.2: *"Variables are not limited to x; instruction includes using a variety of lowercase letters for their variables, however o, i, and l should be avoided as they too closely resemble zero and one."*
5. **Do not hand students the Laws of Exponents.** MA.8.AR.1.1: *"Students should not be told the properties but rather should derive them through experience and reason."* and *"Teach one law at a time to allow for conceptual understanding instead of memorizing the rules."* Echoed at MA.8.NSO.1.3.
6. **Point-slope and standard form are out of scope.** MA.8.AR.3.3 and 3.4: *"Point-slope form and standard forms are not expectations at this grade level."*
7. **Do not compare two lines' slopes for mastery of AR.3.5.** *"For mastery of this benchmark, it is not the expectation to compare slopes or y-intercepts of two linear equations in two variables."*
8. **Do not use the distance formula.** MA.8.GR.1.2 Clarification 2.
9. **Function notation f(x) is Algebra 1**, not grade 8. MA.8.F.1.1.
10. **Do not teach the areas of emphasis as units, or in printed order.** Front matter: *"Student learning and instruction should not focus on the stated areas of emphasis as individual units"* and *"Benchmarks within the areas of emphasis should not be taught within the order in which they appear. To do so would strip the progression of mathematical ideas."* ⚠ *These two sentences came through a summarizing layer and rendered slightly differently across fetches — re-verify against p. 13 before quoting them in a teacher's edition.*

**Absent at grade 8, present at grade 7:** an explicit search of all 40 benchmarks found **no** grade 8 statement about cross-multiplication, no "don't demand simplest form" caution, and no "don't force a solution order" caution. Treat those as **not carried forward**, not as unsearched — but note the PDF-side sweep only covered pages 1–50.

### Terminology Florida wants used

- **Slope:** *"Instruction includes using a variety of vocabulary… To describe the slope, one can say either 'the vertical change divided by the horizontal change' or 'rise over run.'"* (repeated at AR.3.2, 3.3, 3.5) — **both phrasings are sanctioned; do not pick one and ban the other.**
- **Functions:** input = **independent variable**, output = **dependent variable**. *"Vocabulary is important in this benchmark as it connects to future learning related to domain and range."*
- **Inequalities:** *"Students should explore how 'is greater than or equal to' and 'is strictly greater than' are similar and different."*
- **Equation formats:** give linear equations in forms other than y = mx + b — *y = ax + b*, *y = b + mx*, *h = 0.21m + 4.9*.
- **Transformations:** informal language (turns, flips, slides) transitions to formal terms. **Prime notation marks the image; the preimage never carries primes.**
- **Probability:** **P(event) notation** at MA.8.DP.2.2 and 2.3 — same as grade 7.
- **Calculators:** MA.8.NSO.1.4 — *"When using calculators to represent very large and very small numbers with an exponent indicated as 'E', instruction relates the number following 'E' as the power of 10."* **This is a named instructional expectation, not an aside** — see Part 8.

## PART 7 — REVISION RULES FOR MATH NATION 7 ACCELERATED

1. **Keep the math identical.** Only language changes unless we agree otherwise.
2. **Tag every problem with its benchmark code**, and mark whether it is **grade 7 or grade 8** content. Math Nation 7 Accelerated mixes them lesson by lesson.
3. **Flag off-blueprint lessons.** If a lesson sits on a benchmark the course drops (Part 0), say so in the defect list and let Croix decide. Do not cut it silently.
4. **Check every problem against its benchmark's boundaries.** Grade 8 has a lot of them and they are unusually specific: radicands ≤ 225, perfect cubes ±125, scientific-notation addition within 2 exponents, rotations only 90/180/270/360 about the origin, reflections only over axis-parallel lines, dilations only centered at the origin, systems solved **by graphing only**, repeated experiments **twice only**, scatter plots ≤ 20 points, no point-slope, no standard form, no function notation, no distance formula. **A Math Nation item that exceeds these is a curriculum decision to flag, not a wording fix.**
5. **One question per question.** Split buried multi-part asks into (a), (b), (c) — which matches FAST multi-interaction format anyway. No bubble-in-a-story items.
6. **Front-load the ask**, then the data. Use *"An expression is shown."* and its siblings.
7. **Active voice, short sentences, no throat-clearing.**
8. **Keep necessary context.** Trimming to a keyword hunt is exactly what Florida calls a misconception.
9. **Don't strip the distractor-bait.** If a problem is built to catch a flipped Δy/Δx or a hypotenuse misidentification, that difficulty stays.
10. **Match state notation:** P(event); y = mx + b; independent/dependent variable; prime notation on the image only.
11. **Flag anything genuinely ambiguous** rather than picking an interpretation quietly.
12. **Don't manufacture arithmetic difficulty — but for accelerated, retune less readily and say why.** In the on-level project several values were retuned because they produced answers like 526/2475 that no on-level student could produce or check. **This bar moves for accelerated.** These students can and should handle a value that is merely tedious. The test is no longer "is this ugly?" — it is:
    - Does the ugliness teach anything? *(Radicands that are not perfect squares teach estimation. Keep them.)*
    - Does it exceed a stated benchmark boundary? *(A radicand over 225 does. Retune it.)*
    - Does it hide the concept behind the arithmetic? *(A slope of 47/93 tests division, not slope. Retune it.)*
    - **When I do retune, I now say so and give the reason in the "Changes from the Lesson" section.** Silent retuning is what I want to avoid here, not retuning itself.
13. **Weight practice by the released-item data.** The six sub-27% skills in Part 4 get more room, not less, even for an accelerated group. Four of the six are fluency, and fluency is the thing acceleration most often shortchanges.
14. **Watch for the two "Also Assesses" pairings.** Practice MA.8.AR.3.1 inside function questions and MA.8.DP.2.2 inside real-world prediction questions, because that is how they will be tested.

- 5/11 displays as **0.454545455** — the true value is 0.454545454… The final 5 is rounding, not part of the pattern.
- 1/3 displays as **0.333333333** — here the rounding is invisible, which is why students trust the screen.

**Teaching rule:** read the pattern from the *middle* of the display, never the last digit. **Desmos scientific** shows more digits and does not round the same way, so the two disagree on screen. Say so out loud rather than letting students discover it and decide one is broken.

This attaches to **MA.7.NSO.1.2**, which the course carries.

#### New at grade 8: the "E" on the display — this is a standards issue

MA.8.NSO.1.4's instructional guide states: *"When using calculators to represent very large and very small numbers with an exponent indicated as 'E', instruction relates the number following 'E' as the power of 10."* And Florida names as a misconception that **students read the "E" as an error message.**

So the scientific-notation lessons need an explicit calculator moment: `4.2E9` means 4.2 × 10⁹, not an error. **The TI-30XA and Desmos display this differently** — plan to show both. Same teaching posture as the rounding rule: say it out loud before they find it.

#### Assessment policy — DECIDED, and it is one sentence

> **Calculators are allowed on everything. Nothing about calculators is printed on any page. No exceptions.**

Croix, 2 September 2026:

> Calculators: allowed on every assessment. Print nothing about calculators on any test page. […] I announce the policy in class; the paper stays clean.

That is the whole policy. It applies to assessments, quizzes, reviews, practice tests, worksheets and slides alike: **no page tells a student whether they may pick up a calculator**, because a page that grants permission and a page that withholds it are both printing the policy, and only one voice states it — hers, out loud, in the room.

It carries a design obligation, and this is the part to actually remember. **If a question stops measuring anything once a calculator is on the desk, the question is wrong, not the policy.** Rewrite it so the reasoning is the graded thing. The unit assessment's question 2 is the worked example: "which two integers does it lie between" is a display read, so it now asks for the comparison the bracket came from — the perfect squares or cubes, or the value of π — which a calculator supplies arithmetic for and still does not choose. Worksheet questions that estimate do the same by asking for the perfect squares in part a before the root in part b.
