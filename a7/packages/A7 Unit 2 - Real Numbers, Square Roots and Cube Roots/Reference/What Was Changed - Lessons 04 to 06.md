# Unit 2, Lessons 4–6 — what I found before building

Grade 7 Accelerated · 2.4 Approximating Irrational Numbers · 2.5 Rational and Irrational Numbers on the Number Line · 2.6 Plotting, Ordering, and Comparing Real Numbers

I worked every comparison, every ordering and every approximation in the three teacher's editions, the three homework sheets and the three practice sheets, and checked every printed number line against the values it claims to show. Everything below is verified.

**Headline: the mathematics in these three lessons is much cleaner than in Lessons 1–3.** Every comparison in 2.5 and 2.6 is correct. Every ordering is correct. The problems here are one arithmetic slip, four wording errors, a set of items that are unanswerable by any method the lesson teaches, and structure.

---

## 1. Math Nation now contradicts itself — and Lesson 4 agrees with us

This is the most useful thing in this batch.

In **2.4.2**, Damion writes four equations and students judge them:

| | Equation | Math Nation's key |
|---|---|---|
| A | √64 = 8 | **correct** |
| B | ∛64 = 4 | correct |
| C | √−64 = −8 | incorrect |
| D | ∛−64 = −4 | correct |

Equation A is marked **correct** — √64 = 8, one value, the positive one. That is the opposite of what their Lesson 2 taught, where √64 = ±8 appears on the guided instruction page, on both practice sheets and throughout the key.

Their teacher note even anticipates the collision: *"Some students may also identify Equation A as an incorrect answer because only one of the two solutions is listed. Consider using this as an opportunity to discuss why perfect squares have two roots but perfect cubes only have one."* In other words, a student who learned their Lesson 2 properly will now get 2.4.2 wrong, and the teacher is asked to talk them out of it.

Then **2.4.4** introduces the term formally: *"The principal square root is the positive square root of a positive real number,"* listed as a Key Term for the lesson.

**What this means for your class.** Your students learned the correct definition in Lesson 2 four days ago. They will walk into 2.4.2 and agree with the key without a discussion, and 2.4.4 becomes a naming — here is the word for the thing you already know — rather than a correction. Nothing in Lessons 4–6 needs changing on this point.

One small repair: their definition says *"the positive square root of a positive real number,"* which leaves out zero. √0 = 0, and 0 is not positive. The word is **non-negative**, and our Lesson 2 worksheet already has √0 = 0 on it.

## 2. Mathematical errors

**2.4.6, the value of 2·√6 is wrong.** The table keys it at **4.8**. The true value is 2 × 2.44949 = **4.89898**, which is 4.9 to the nearest tenth. The 4.8 comes from approximating √6 as 2.4 first and then doubling — and their own instruction says to do exactly that — but the answer is still off by a tenth, and **the printed number line on the same page plots the point at about 4.9**, so the table and the picture disagree with each other.

This matters beyond the one item: it is the only place in the lesson where approximate-then-operate and operate-then-approximate give different answers to the nearest tenth. That is worth teaching on purpose rather than getting wrong by accident.

**Additional Practice 7A.02.04, questions 4 and 6 ask the wrong question.**

- Q4 as printed: *"Between which two perfect squares does **√95** lie?"* √95 lies between 9 and 10, and neither is a perfect square. The question means **95**, which lies between 81 and 100.
- Q6 as printed: *"Between which two perfect cubes does **∛141** lie?"* Same error — it means 141, between 125 and 216.

The homework sheet asks both correctly (*"between which two perfect squares does 23 lie"*), so this is a slip on the practice sheet only.

**Two symbol slips in teacher-facing notes.**

- 2.4.4's scaffolding questions ask *"which cube root **√9** is closest to"* — it means ∛9. As written it says √9, which is exactly 3 and has nothing to approximate.
- 2.6.2's key note reads *"The **∛49** is approximately −3.7."* ∛49 is about +3.66. It means −∛49. The comparison itself is keyed correctly.

## 3. Items that cannot be answered by the method the lesson teaches

The whole unit teaches estimation: find the two perfect squares or cubes it sits between, decide which it is closer to, test a decimal or two. Several ordering items are built from numbers too close together for that method to separate.

| Item | The numbers | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Practice 7A.02.06, Q10** | ∛−126 = −5.01330 · −5.01̄ = −5.01111 · −√25 = −5.00000 | **0.013 across all three** |
| Practice 7A.02.05, Q9 | 7⅞ = 7.8750 · √63 = 7.9373 | 0.062 |
| Homework 7A.02.06, Q9 | 123/15 = 8.2000 · √70 = 8.3666 · 3π−1 = 8.4248 | 0.22 |
| Homework 7A.02.05, Q7 | ½π = 1.5708 · 1⅗ = 1.6000 | 0.029 |
| Practice 7A.02.06, Q2 | −28/6 = −4.66667 · ∛−100 = −4.64159 | 0.025 |
| 2.4.4, question 2 | ∛8 = 2 · ∛9 = 2.080 · ∛10 = 2.154 | 0.154, all three labelled on one line |

The worst of these is Practice 7A.02.06 question 10. To order those three a student needs four decimal places on a cube root of a non-perfect cube. There is no estimation strategy that gets there, and the sheet then asks them to plot the result. The item tests calculator button-pressing, not the benchmark.

Question 9 on the same sheet is nearly as bad: 78/4 = 19.5, 2π² = 19.7392, √400 = 20 — and the middle one is two operations deep.

## 4. Cubes the students were never asked to learn

MA.8.AR.2.3 caps perfect cubes at −125 to 125, and that is the list Lesson 3 taught: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125. MA.8.NSO.1.2 — the benchmark for Lessons 5 and 6 — states no cap, so none of these items is out of bounds. But several of them require a perfect cube nobody asked students to know:

| Item | Needs | Which is |
|---|---|---|
| Practice 7A.02.04 Q6–8 | ∛141, so 125 and **216** | 6³ |
| 2.4.4 Q2 | ∛140, so 125 and **216** | 6³ |
| Homework 7A.02.06 Q4 | ∛−216 | 6³ |
| Homework 7A.02.06 Q7 | ∛530, so **512** and **729** | 8³ and 9³ |

Seven items across the two Lesson 6 sheets and the Lesson 4 practice sheet. A student working from the list they were given cannot start any of them.

## 5. Item design

**Homework 7A.02.05, questions 1 through 6 all have the same answer.** Every one of the six comparisons is **<**:

| | Left | Right | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ∛30 = 3.107 | 7/2 = 3.5 | < |
| 2 | −14.123… | −2√49 = −14 | < |
| 3 | 3π = 9.425 | π²+1 = 10.870 | < |
| 4 | −√64 = −8 | ∛−64 = −4 | < |
| 5 | √111 = 10.536 | 11 2/9 = 11.222 | < |
| 6 | 29/6 = 4.833 | 4.9̄ = 5 | < |

A student who writes `<` six times without reading scores 6/6. The practice sheet for the same lesson is built properly — its six answers run `< = > > < >`, including a genuine equality (π/2 − 3 versus −3 + ½π) — so the fix is to make the homework match the practice sheet's design.

Two smaller things: **Homework 7A.02.06 question 10** writes −5 15/30 rather than −5½, and **Practice 7A.02.05 question 9** writes 98/−11 with the negative in the denominator. Neither is wrong, both are odd.

## 6. Structure

**Partner and group work: 14 printed instances, plus two whole components built on it.**

| Lesson | Printed instances | Named routine | The hard one |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.4 | 7 | Think-Pair-Share | 2.4.2 is a "Collaboration" and 2.4.4 is a partner Exploration with "compare your answers to your partner's" built in |
| 2.5 | 4 | Which One Doesn't Belong | **2.5.4 Activity** — cards handed out, students move to a "rational" or "irrational" side of the room, split into groups by range, then order and plot together |
| 2.6 | 3 | Number Talk | **The entire lesson is three rotating stations**, and the teacher note says to assign partners within each group |

2.5.4 and the whole of 2.6 are the two biggest rebuilds in Unit 2 so far. Lesson 6 as written has no notes, no examples and no teacher-led instruction at all — it is three stations and a reflection.

**All three wrap-ups produce nothing gradeable.** 2.4.7 asks students to *"explain the process for approximating an irrational number,"* keyed "answers will vary." 2.5.6 asks for *"a summary of what you learned,"* keyed "answers will vary." 2.6.5 asks students to reflect on the unit learning targets.

**Two of the three warm-ups are worth keeping.**

- **2.4.1** is a number-pyramid puzzle. The arithmetic works (2+10=12, 10+3=13, 3+6=9, 12+13=25, 13+9=22, 25+22=47), but it has nothing to do with irrational numbers. Same category as the magic square in Lesson 3.
- **2.5.1 is good.** Which One Doesn't Belong with 3.14, 22/7, π and 3.1̄4̄ — four numbers where every one has a defensible case, and the whole point is rational versus irrational. I would keep this.
- **2.6.1 is good.** Six real numbers to sort, order and plot. Real mathematics, on-benchmark, individual as written.

**Calculators.** Lesson 4's materials note says: *"Students will need access to a calculator for 2.4.3 Guided Instruction, but then calculators should not be used for the remainder of this lesson or for the subsequent lessons."* That is a defensible instructional choice — estimation is the skill — but it is a blanket ban across three lessons, and it cuts against your standing policy. See the questions below.

## 7. What is worth keeping

- **2.4.2 Damion's four equations.** The best item in this batch, and it is the payoff of our Lesson 2. Four statements, three true, and the false one is false for a reason students can state.
- **2.5.1 Which One Doesn't Belong.** Four numbers, four defensible answers, exactly on benchmark.
- **2.5.2's ordering set** — 0.125, −√12, −8, −√49, −2π, ∛−8, −13/3. Well built: −√49 and ∛−8 look irrational and are not, which is the whole skill.
- **2.6.1 Bell Work** and **2.6.2 Station 1's six comparisons** — the comparison set runs `< = < < > <` with one exact equality and one near-miss, which is how a comparison set should be built.
- **2.6.4 Sara's error analysis.** Sara estimates ∛11 using perfect *squares* and lands on 3.5 instead of 2.2. It is the single most likely error on this benchmark and the item catches it cleanly.
