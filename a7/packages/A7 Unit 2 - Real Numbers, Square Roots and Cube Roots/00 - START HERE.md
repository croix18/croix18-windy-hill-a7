## How the files are named

**`A7  <unit>.<lesson>` — two spaces — `<what it is>`**

`A7 2.03  Worksheet` is Accelerated grade 7, Unit 2, Lesson 3, the worksheet. This matches the
on-level set exactly, so the two courses sit side by side in the drive without looking like they
came from different places.

Lesson numbers are **two digits**. `A7 1.10` has to sort after `A7 1.09`, and Drive and Classroom
sort text rather than numbers, so a single digit would put Lesson 10 between Lessons 1 and 2.

Unit-wide documents drop the lesson number: `A7 1  Unit Review`, `A7 1  Unit Assessment`,
`A7 1  Reference Sheet`. A space sorts before a full stop, so those sit at the top of their folder.

## The square-root question, and why Lesson 2.02 is rebuilt

Math Nation's Lesson 2 defines `√64 = ±8` throughout. That breaks three benchmarks the course
carries — MA.8.NSO.1.7 (radicals in order of operations), MA.8.NSO.1.2 (plotting roots on a number
line) and MA.8.GR.1.1 (a hypotenuse). Lesson 2.02 is built around the correct distinction instead,
and its teacher's edition opens with a **"Read This First"** section explaining the change.

**Their own Lesson 4 agrees with us.** In their 2.4.2 Damion writes `√64 = 8` and the key marks it
correct; their 2.4.4 then introduces "principal square root" as a Key Term. Their Lesson 2 and
their Lesson 4 contradict each other. Your students learn the correct definition first, so 2.4.2
lands without an argument.

## Lesson 2.06 is a practice day, built differently

Math Nation's Lesson 6 has no notes, no examples and no teacher-led instruction — it is three
rotating stations plus a reflection, with assigned partners inside each group.

Rebuilt for a class with no group work: **40 minutes of review and worked examples from the front,
then a 13-minute four-part circuit each student works alone** — the split is in the timing table
below, and it is read from the deck rather than typed here. Every question from all three
stations is on the circuit. What is gone is the rotating, the grouping and the station cards.

## Standing decisions baked into these files

- **No partner or group work anywhere.** Fourteen printed instances across Lessons 2.04–2.06 alone,
  plus two whole components built on group work — Lesson 2.05's card sort and the whole of 2.06.
- **It is a worksheet, never homework.** Students start it in class; what is left goes home.
- **Calculators: allowed on everything, and nothing about them is printed on any page.**
  *Croix ruled this on 2 September, superseding his 1 September ruling.* "Calculators: allowed on
  every assessment. Print nothing about calculators on any test page. I announce the policy in
  class; the paper stays clean." A calculator line is any instruction telling a student whether
  they may use one on this page — a permission and a prohibition are equally a line, and both are
  out, on assessments, reviews, worksheets and slides alike. The obligation this creates is on the
  QUESTION: an item that stops measuring anything once a calculator is on the desk gets rewritten
  so the reasoning is what is graded. Unit assessment question 2 asks for the comparison the
  bracket came from and not only the bracket; Lessons 2.04's worksheets ask for the perfect squares
  or cubes by name before the root.
  *(Superseded, kept as history: the 1 September ruling put a header reading "You may use your
  calculator, except on the questions that say otherwise" on the assessment and "No calculator on
  this question — estimate" on its questions 1, 2 and 8. All of that is gone from the paper. Do not
  build from it. Describing what the FAST platform provides is a different thing and belongs on the
  reference sheet.)*
- **Old-school textbook styling.** No commentary on the slides, no clip art.
- **Every key is generated from the same source file as the student sheet.**

## Before the week starts

- **Lesson 2.04 has the one arithmetic error in the batch.** Math Nation keys 2·√6 at 4.8; it is
  4.9, and their own number line on the same page plots it at 4.9. That contradiction is now the
  named-difficulty slide, taught as "round last, not first."
- **Lesson 2.05's homework had all six comparisons come out `<`.** A student could score 6/6
  without reading. Rebuilt to `< > > < = <`, with one genuine equality.
- **Several ordering items were retuned.** Their worst asked students to order −5.0133, −5.0111 and
  −5.0000 and then plot the result. Every ordering on these sheets now has gaps of at least a tenth.
- **Cube roots came back to the taught list.** Core items use the Lesson 2.03 list; 216, 343 and
  512 appear only in questions marked **STRETCH**, which say so in print.
- **The assessment is not Math Nation's.** Nine questions, **34 points — one point per lettered
  part**, per Croix's ruling of 1 September; the scoring sheet is one row per part and the paper is
  out of however many rows it has. Theirs had one false
  statement, totalled 98 points while claiming 100, decided two items in the second decimal place,
  and never tested the principal square root at all. `Reference/What Was Changed - Unit 2
  Assessment.md` lists all eighteen defects and what was done about each.
