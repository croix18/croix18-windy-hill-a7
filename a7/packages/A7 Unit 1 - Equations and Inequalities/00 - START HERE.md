Windy Hill Middle School · course 1205050 · ten lessons, a review, an assessment.

---

## How the files are named

**`A7  <unit>.<lesson>` — two spaces — `<what it is>`**

`A7 2.03  Worksheet` is Accelerated grade 7, Unit 2, Lesson 3, the worksheet. This matches the
on-level set exactly, so the two courses sit side by side in the drive without looking like they
came from different places.

Lesson numbers are **two digits**. `A7 1.10` has to sort after `A7 1.09`, and Drive and Classroom
sort text rather than numbers, so a single digit would put Lesson 10 between Lessons 1 and 2.

Unit-wide documents drop the lesson number: `A7 1  Unit Review`, `A7 1  Unit Assessment`,
`A7 1  Reference Sheet`. A space sorts before a full stop, so those sit at the top of their folder.

## Standing decisions baked into these files

- **No partner or group work anywhere.** Every collaboration, card sort, gallery walk and
  Think-Pair-Share in the Math Nation materials was rebuilt as individual work — 34 instances
  across the unit.
- **It is a worksheet, never homework.** Nothing on any student page carries the word. Students
  start it in class; what is left goes home.
- **Calculators: TI-30XA and Desmos.** Allowed on the assessment, but no student page says so —
  announce it. Math Nation's assessment guide recommends the opposite; see below.
- **Old-school textbook styling.** No commentary on the slides, no "write this exactly" notes,
  no clip art.
- **Every key is generated from the same source file as the student sheet,** so they cannot disagree.

## Before the week starts

- **Lesson 1.03 is the long one.** Math Nation's own component times total 55 minutes before any
  notes or worksheet. The plan fits 53 because the Exploration is compressed into Notes II and
  Example 3. Read that teacher's edition before you plan the day. *Segments are named rather than
  numbered here on purpose: the decks were renumbered once already and every hand-typed slide
  number in the teacher's editions went stale at the same moment.*
- **Lessons 1.01–1.07 are seven grade 7 lessons in a row.** That is by design, not drift — this
  class owes a whole grade 7 course alongside the grade 8 one.
- **The assessment is twelve questions and 21 points.** *One point per lettered part* — Croix's
  ruling of 1 September: every touch point from a student counts 1, and the paper is out of
  however many it has. The three benchmarks land on 7, 7 and 7. Grading is a tick per part and a
  count: no arithmetic, no partial credit to weigh. The student copy has a `Score ____ / 21` line
  and its point values; the teacher's copy carries each question's benchmark in the right margin
  and opens with a per-benchmark score tracker. **A wrong setup is not charged twice** — a student
  who solves their own wrong equation correctly still earns the next part.
- **One conflict to know about.** Math Nation's assessment guide twice recommends withholding
  calculators. That is the opposite of your policy and of the Grade 8 FAST, which allows one on
  every item. Questions 1a, 6 and 7 are meaningfully harder without one.
